## Understand the Three Work Areas

### 1. Data Solutions Engineer

A simple lifecycle answer:

1. Clarify the business question, users, decisions, measures, service level, and data sensitivity.
2. Identify sources, ownership, access, grain, keys, refresh frequency, and expected volume.
3. Ingest data through an approved batch, streaming, file, database, or API pattern.
4. Clean and transform it using documented, repeatable logic.
5. Model data for analytics, often with fact and dimension structures where appropriate.
6. Validate completeness, uniqueness, validity, consistency, timeliness, and reconciliation totals.
7. Serve governed data through a semantic layer, dataset, dashboard, report, or application.
8. Deploy through controlled environments, test, document, monitor, and improve.

Use this as learned methodology. Your verified work supports SQL extraction, relational modelling, transformation logic, validation, and reporting planning—not production cloud pipelines.

### 2. Business Analyst — Digitisation and Automation

1. Define the problem, customer or employee pain point, baseline, scope, and desired outcome.
2. Map the current process, actors, systems, handoffs, decisions, exceptions, controls, and delays.
3. Separate business, functional, data, security, compliance, and non-functional requirements.
4. Convert needs into prioritized requirements or user stories with acceptance criteria.
5. Compare solution options by value, feasibility, risk, effort, dependency, and maintainability.
6. Support test scenarios, defect triage, change impact, training, communication, and adoption.
7. Track benefits against the agreed baseline after implementation.

Your direct evidence is requirements gathering, process mapping, documentation, stakeholder communication, and Agile participation. User-story ownership, formal testing, change readiness, and benefits tracking are development areas.

### 3. Developer — Automation and Digital Solutions

Know the purpose of the tools without claiming experience:

- **Power Apps:** low-code applications for structured business workflows.
- **Power Automate:** event- or schedule-driven workflows across systems and approvals.
- **Power BI:** semantic modelling, measures, interactive reporting, and governed sharing.
- **Azure:** cloud platform containing data, integration, AI, compute, storage, identity, and monitoring services.

- **Copilot / Azure AI:** AI-enabled assistance or applications that require grounding, testing, security, and responsible-use controls.

## Data Quality and Governance

Be ready to distinguish:

- **Completeness:** required values are present.
- **Uniqueness:** records that should be unique are not duplicated.
- **Validity:** values follow allowed formats, ranges, and business rules.
- **Consistency:** the same concept agrees across fields or systems.
- **Accuracy:** data correctly represents the real-world event or approved source.
- **Timeliness:** data is current enough for the decision.
- **Integrity:** relationships and constraints remain valid.
- **Lineage:** traceable path from source through transformations to output.

Financial data also requires least-privilege access, secure handling, retention rules, auditability, approved definitions, and controlled changes. Do not invent familiarity with Manulife's internal policies; say you would learn and follow them.

## SQL Topics to Practise

- `INNER JOIN` versus `LEFT JOIN` and how joins can unintentionally duplicate rows.
- Aggregation, `GROUP BY`, `HAVING`, `CASE`, date logic, and null handling.
- CTEs and window functions such as `ROW_NUMBER`, `RANK`, `SUM OVER`, and `LAG`.
- Primary keys, foreign keys, uniqueness constraints, and validation queries.
- Finding duplicates, missing values, orphan records, invalid statuses, and reconciliation differences.
- Query readability, documented assumptions, and checking the grain before aggregation.

## AI-Enabled Solution Framework

When proposing AI, cover both value and control:

1. Define the user, decision, baseline, and measurable benefit.
2. Confirm that AI is appropriate compared with deterministic rules or ordinary automation.
3. Use approved data and protect personal, financial, and confidential information.
4. Establish grounding, permissions, human review, fallback, and escalation.
5. Test accuracy, harmful or biased outputs, edge cases, robustness, and user experience.
6. Log versions, prompts or rules, evaluation results, incidents, and changes where appropriate.
7. Pilot with a limited group, measure results, collect feedback, and monitor after release.

Your projects support AI-assisted interpretation of validated SQL results. They do not establish production AI application development.
