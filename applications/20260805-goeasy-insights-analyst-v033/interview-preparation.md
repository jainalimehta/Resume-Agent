## Consumer-Lending Concepts to Learn

Use these as interview preparation, not claims of prior experience:

- **Application volume:** Number of applications received in a defined period and channel.
- **Approval rate:** Approved applications divided by eligible completed applications; clarify policy and denominator.
- **Booking or funding rate:** Funded accounts divided by approvals or applications, depending on the business question.
- **Average loan size:** Funded principal divided by funded accounts; segment by product, channel, geography, and risk band.
- **Delinquency rate:** Accounts or balances past due under a defined aging threshold; specify account-based versus balance-based.
- **Net charge-off rate:** Net charged-off balance relative to an agreed receivables measure; use the company's approved definition.
- **Payment rate:** Payments received relative to statement balance, amount due, or receivables; define consistently.
- **Customer acquisition cost:** Eligible acquisition spend divided by acquired or funded customers.
- **Repeat-customer rate:** Customers returning for another eligible product or transaction divided by the relevant cohort.
- **Customer lifetime value:** Expected customer contribution over a defined horizon; depends on revenue, losses, servicing cost, retention, and discounting assumptions.
- **Conversion funnel:** Application, completion, approval, acceptance, funding, activation, and repayment stages, with clear exclusions.

## Insight-Development Framework

When given a business question:

1. Clarify the decision, stakeholder, time horizon, and success measure.
2. Define the population, grain, metrics, business rules, and comparison period.
3. Profile and validate the data before analysis.
4. Segment results by meaningful dimensions such as product, channel, risk band, geography, tenure, and customer cohort.
5. Separate correlation from causation and test alternative explanations.
6. Quantify the opportunity and state assumptions and limitations.
7. Recommend an action, owner, expected direction of impact, and monitoring metric.
8. Present the headline first, supporting evidence second, and technical detail last.

## Likely Case Questions

### Approval rate falls while application volume remains stable. What would you investigate?

1. Validate definitions, reporting periods, data completeness, duplicates, and status mappings.
2. Determine whether the change is concentrated by product, channel, geography, customer segment, risk tier, or time.
3. Check applicant mix, missing information, policy changes, decision-engine changes, fraud controls, and operational backlog.
4. Compare downstream funding, loss, and customer outcomes before recommending a looser or tighter process.
5. Present the primary drivers, limitations, options, risks, and monitoring plan.

### A dashboard shows improving originations but worsening delinquency. How would you explain it?

Compare cohorts at consistent account ages, control for product and customer mix, examine approval and pricing changes, separate vintage effects from calendar effects, and avoid comparing immature new accounts with seasoned portfolios. Explain the growth-versus-risk trade-off and recommend cohort monitoring rather than a conclusion based on one aggregate trend.

### How would you improve a recurring report?

Start with the decisions it supports. Remove unused metrics, define a clear KPI hierarchy, reconcile totals, add trend and target context, make exceptions visible, document definitions, automate repeatable transformations where appropriate, and confirm with users that the revised report changes or accelerates a real decision.

## Data Storytelling Structure

Use a compact four-part narrative:

1. **What happened?** State the metric movement and period.
2. **Why did it happen?** Identify the strongest verified drivers and segments.
3. **Why does it matter?** Connect to customer, operational, financial, or risk outcomes.
4. **What should we do?** Recommend an action, owner, timing, and KPI to monitor.

Always distinguish facts, interpretations, and recommendations.
