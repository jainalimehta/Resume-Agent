## Business-application implementation lifecycle

Use this structure when asked how you would support an implementation:

1. Discover: clarify objectives, stakeholders, scope, pain points, constraints, policies, and success measures.
2. Map: document current-state roles, process steps, handoffs, data, systems, controls, and exceptions.
3. Define: convert needs into prioritized functional and non-functional requirements with acceptance criteria.
4. Fit-gap: compare requirements with standard product capabilities and identify configuration, process change, integration, data, reporting, or extension needs.
5. Design: define the future-state process, roles, security, data, interfaces, reports, controls, and change impacts.
6. Configure: apply approved settings in controlled environments with traceable decisions and version discipline.
7. Validate: prepare test cases, reconcile data, record defects, retest fixes, and support user acceptance testing.
8. Enable: create role-based instructions, train users with realistic scenarios, and confirm readiness.
9. Deploy: follow cutover, approval, communication, contingency, and support plans.
10. Stabilize: triage issues, monitor adoption and controls, document lessons, and transition ownership.

## Requirements workshop approach

Do not claim prior facilitation. Explain how you would prepare and support one:

- Confirm the objective, participants, pre-read, decisions required, and outputs.
- Research the client's industry and current process before the session.
- Use open questions first, then probe roles, volumes, exceptions, controls, pain points, data, and measures.
- Separate stated preferences from underlying business needs.
- Play back what you heard, test assumptions, and capture decisions, owners, and open issues.
- Convert notes into process maps and requirements, then circulate them for validation.
