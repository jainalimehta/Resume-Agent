### How would you investigate a payroll discrepancy?

1. Confirm the employee, pay period, issue, amount, urgency, and confidentiality requirements.
2. Review authorized source records such as employment/position data, schedule/time entry, leave, rate, collective-agreement provision, adjustment history, and system audit trail.
3. Compare the expected calculation with payroll and labour-distribution outputs.
4. Identify whether the cause is source data, timing, configuration, interface, manual entry, approval, or interpretation.
5. Consult the appropriate payroll, HR, manager, technology, or labour-relations specialist.
6. Document evidence, action, approval, correction, communication, and follow-up validation.

State that this is a studied control approach, not prior professional payroll-case ownership.

### How would you reconcile payroll or leave-bank reports?

Confirm the reconciliation objective and authoritative sources; compare beginning balance, approved activity, adjustments, and ending balance; use employee/position and pay-period keys; identify missing, duplicate, late, or inconsistent entries; investigate exceptions; document corrections and approvals; rerun the report; preserve an audit trail; and obtain sign-off.

### How would you respond to an employee inquiry?

Verify identity and authority, listen carefully, restate the issue, avoid assumptions, protect personal information, consult the correct agreement/policy/system record, explain confirmed facts in plain language, give a realistic follow-up time, document the interaction, escalate when interpretation or approval is outside your authority, and close the loop.

### How would you apply a collective agreement?

Identify the employee group and applicable agreement; find the exact article, definitions, exceptions, letters of understanding, and effective dates; review relevant City policy and legislation; confirm facts; avoid relying on memory; document the interpretation; and escalate ambiguous or precedent-setting cases to labour relations or management. Never claim professional interpretation experience.

## Payroll Fundamentals to Study

### Gross-to-Net Flow

- Employee and position data, approved time and attendance, earnings, premiums, overtime, leave, and retroactive changes feed gross pay.
- Statutory and authorized deductions may include income tax, CPP, EI, benefits, pension, union dues, garnishments, or other approved items.
- Net pay, employer contributions, accounting distributions, remittances, records, and reports must reconcile.
- Actual treatment depends on legislation, collective agreements, City policy, employee status, and system configuration.

### Key Controls

- Segregation of duties and authorized approvals
- Valid employee, position, rate, schedule, and cost-centre data
- Interface and upload control totals
- Duplicate, missing, late, and out-of-range transaction checks
- Payroll register, variance, exception, and labour-distribution reviews
- Leave-bank and retroactive-adjustment reconciliation
- Restricted access, privacy, retention, and audit trails
- Correction approval, rerun validation, and documented employee communication

### SAP and SuccessFactors Concepts

- Learn employee master data, organizational units, jobs, positions, reporting relationships, effective-dated changes, workflows, roles, and audit history.
- Understand that SuccessFactors may supply employee/position events while SAP Payroll performs payroll processing, depending on the City's integration.
- Study manual entry versus electronic upload controls, interface monitoring, rejected records, effective dates, retroactivity, and reconciliation.
- Do not claim knowledge of the City's configuration.

### Complement and Organization Management

- Complement describes approved staffing positions and related budget/organizational controls.
- Position records may include organizational unit, job, status, incumbent, reporting relationship, cost assignment, and effective dates.
- Common checks include vacant/filled status, duplicate incumbency, position authorization, funding, effective dates, and alignment across HR, payroll, budget, and organization charts.
