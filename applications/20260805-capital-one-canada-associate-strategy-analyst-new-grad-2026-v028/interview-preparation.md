## Strategy Case Framework

For a product, pricing, customer, or growth problem:

1. **Clarify the objective:** Customer outcome, financial outcome, timeframe, decision owner, constraints, and success metric.
2. **Define the population:** Eligible customers, product, channel, geography, time period, exclusions, and unit of analysis.
3. **Build the economics:** Revenue drivers, costs, losses, risk, acquisition cost, retention, and expected value.
4. **Segment:** Customer profile, behaviour, risk, channel, tenure, product, and response history.
5. **Form hypotheses:** State what might drive the outcome and what evidence would support or reject it.
6. **Test with data:** Check quality, definitions, bias, comparison groups, sensitivity, and alternative explanations.
7. **Develop options:** Expected value, customer impact, risk, feasibility, legal or policy constraints, and operational effort.
8. **Recommend:** Choose an option, state assumptions, risks, guardrails, rollout, and monitoring.
9. **Measure:** Define leading and lagging indicators, test design, review cadence, and stop conditions.

Always separate known facts, assumptions, calculations, and recommendations.

## Sample Case: Credit-Card Offer

Prompt: Capital One is considering a promotional offer for a customer segment. How would you evaluate it?

Strong structure:

- Clarify whether the goal is acquisition, activation, spend, balance growth, retention, or profitability.
- Define the target segment, eligibility rules, channel, offer, timeframe, and comparison group.
- Estimate incremental revenue from interchange, interest, or fees where applicable.
- Estimate incremental costs: rewards, marketing, servicing, funding, fraud, and expected credit loss.
- Consider customer fairness, affordability, disclosure, privacy, compliance, and brand impact.
- Test on a controlled sample where appropriate; define primary and guardrail metrics.
- Monitor activation, spend, response, attrition, delinquency, losses, complaints, and profitability.
- Recommend scale, revise, or stop based on evidence and predefined thresholds.

Do not invent Canadian credit regulations or Capital One policies. State when legal, compliance, or risk experts must validate an option.

## Sample Case: Fraud Trend

Prompt: Fraud losses increased this month. What would you do?

- Validate the metric, reporting delay, denominator, seasonality, and whether the increase is absolute or rate-based.
- Segment by transaction type, channel, merchant, geography, customer tenure, device, amount band, and time.
- Separate first-party and third-party fraud only if definitions are available.
- Identify when the change began and whether it aligns with a product, rule, vendor, or channel change.
- Compare prevented fraud, false positives, customer friction, approval rates, and operational cost.
- Recommend targeted rule or model review with fraud, data science, operations, legal, and technology specialists.
- Pilot carefully and monitor loss reduction alongside customer and approval-rate guardrails.

## Sample Case: New Product

Prompt: Should Capital One launch a new card product?

- Define target customer need and current alternatives.
- Estimate addressable population and acquisition channel.
- Build customer value proposition and unit economics.
- Assess credit, fraud, operational, technology, legal, and reputational risks.
- Identify the minimum viable offer and testable hypotheses.
- Propose a pilot, comparison method, success metrics, guardrails, and learning agenda.
- Recommend based on expected customer value, financial value, risk-adjusted return, and strategic fit.
