## Requirements-Gathering Framework

Start with the problem and decision, not the requested feature.

1. Identify sponsor, users, affected clients, process owner, and technical partners.
2. Clarify current state, pain point, desired outcome, scope, and exclusions.
3. Elicit functional, data, integration, reporting, security, operational, and non-functional needs.
4. Define terms, rules, inputs, outputs, exceptions, dependencies, constraints, and assumptions.
5. Model the process and information flow using an appropriate artifact.
6. Prioritize needs and resolve conflicts with decision owners.
7. Convert requirements into testable statements and acceptance criteria.
8. Review examples or prototypes and obtain documented agreement.
9. Maintain traceability through design, testing, implementation, and change control.

Strong interview phrase:

> A requirement is not complete because it sounds clear; it is complete when the relevant parties share the same interpretation and it can be validated objectively.

## Types of Requirements

- **Business:** outcome or capability the organization needs.
- **Stakeholder/user:** what a user or group must accomplish.
- **Functional:** behaviour the solution must perform.
- **Data:** sources, fields, definitions, quality, retention, and lineage.
- **Integration/interface:** information exchanged between systems, timing, format, and failure handling.
- **Non-functional:** security, privacy, performance, availability, accessibility, scalability, and supportability.
- **Transition:** migration, training, communications, cutover, and temporary needs.

Know these categories conceptually. Do not claim production delivery experience with each.

## Solution-Design Discussion Approach

> I would bring an agreed problem statement, prioritized requirements, process/data flows, constraints, assumptions, and open decisions. I would invite business, architecture, development, security, operations, and QA perspectives as appropriate. I would compare options against client value, feasibility, architecture alignment, risk, cost, delivery effort, supportability, and requirements coverage. I would document decisions and confirm what must be validated next. As an early-career analyst, I would facilitate within my authority and rely on the accountable architect and technical leads for design approval.

## Quality-Assurance and Traceability

Proposed traceability fields:

- requirement ID and description;
- source and business rationale;
- priority and owner;
- related process, rule, interface, or data element;
- design component;
- acceptance criterion and test case;
- status, defect, decision, and change reference;
- approval evidence.

QA approach:

1. Review requirements for ambiguity, duplication, contradiction, and testability.
2. Confirm solution/design coverage for every approved requirement.
3. Derive positive, negative, boundary, exception, and permission scenarios.
4. Validate data and calculations against authoritative sources.
5. Record expected versus actual results and reproducible defects.
6. Re-test fixes and complete regression/UAT support under the approved process.

Your direct evidence is data validation and analytical checks, not production application QA.

## Handling Conflicting Stakeholder Requirements

> I would first restate each underlying need and identify whether the conflict concerns outcome, rule, scope, timing, cost, risk, or solution preference. I would use the agreed business objective and decision authority to compare options, document impacts and trade-offs, and facilitate a decision rather than choosing based on the strongest voice. I would record the outcome, update traceability, and confirm the change with affected stakeholders.

## Client-First Systems Thinking

When assessing a change, ask:

- What client need or friction does this address?
- Which clients, colleagues, channels, and processes are affected?
- Could the design create confusion, delay, exclusion, privacy risk, or inconsistent service?
- What happens when data is missing, systems are unavailable, or a user makes an error?
- How will success and unintended effects be measured?
- Can the process be explained and supported after implementation?
