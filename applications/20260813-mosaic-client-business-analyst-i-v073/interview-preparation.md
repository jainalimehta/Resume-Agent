# Mosaic — Client Business Analyst I Interview Preparation

## 60-Second Introduction

“I am an early-career Business Analytics graduate living in Toronto. At AYLA Solutions in Australia, I gathered reporting requirements, supported SQL extraction and validation, prepared advanced Excel reports, contributed dashboard insights, and participated in Agile planning and reviews. I also designed a Google Forms survey of small businesses, cleaned and analyzed responses in Excel using formulas, filters, pivot tables, percentages, and charts, and documented user-adoption barriers and recommendations. My SQL portfolio adds relational modelling, data quality, KPI analysis, repeatable validation, and cross-domain recommendations. Mosaic interests me because the role connects client reporting, survey research, market insight, and practical decisions.”

## Four Stories to Prepare

1. **Survey design and insight:** Online GST Filing Adoption Research — objective, target respondents, question design, data cleaning, Excel analysis, barriers, and recommendations.
2. **Client reporting:** AYLA — requirements, SQL/Excel support, validation, reporting output, communication, and documentation.
3. **Market research and coordination:** Trans Globe — market-research support, client communication, accurate records, and workflow updates.
4. **Data-quality discipline:** E-Commerce Sales Analytics — relational model, integrity rules, recognized revenue, operational KPIs, and repeatable checks.

## Survey and Market-Testing Framework

1. Define the business decision, target population, hypotheses, and success metrics.
2. Choose the method: survey, observational study, controlled market test, or qualitative research.
3. Avoid leading, double-barrelled, ambiguous, or overlapping questions and response options.
4. Pilot the instrument, confirm consent/privacy requirements, and document sampling limitations.
5. Validate completeness, duplicates, ranges, missingness, and inconsistent categories.
6. Segment results, quantify uncertainty where appropriate, compare groups carefully, and distinguish correlation from causation.
7. Translate findings into a recommendation, testable action, risks, and next measurement.

## Marketing and Client Metrics to Understand

- Reach, impressions, frequency, engagement, and conversion
- Sales lift, incremental sales, baseline, and control groups
- Cost per interaction, lead, acquisition, and conversion
- Distribution, availability, compliance, and share of shelf
- Brand awareness, consideration, intent, and advocacy
- Survey response and completion rates

Do not claim professional experience measuring these. Be ready to define the metric, denominator, time window, source, comparison group, limitations, and decision it supports.

## Technical Questions to Practise

- Explain how you would clean survey responses with inconsistent categories and missing answers.
- Explain `INNER JOIN` versus `LEFT JOIN` for campaign activity and sales results.
- Use conditional aggregation to calculate response or conversion rates by segment.
- Use a window function to rank markets or compare week-over-week performance.
- Explain how to validate a recurring report before distributing it to a client.
- Explain why a sales increase after a campaign does not by itself prove incremental lift.
- Explain sample bias, non-response bias, leading questions, and small-base-size risk.

## Strong Questions for Mosaic

1. Which client decisions and standardized reports would this analyst support most often?
2. What are the main data sources, BI tools, and quality controls used by the team?
3. How does Mosaic distinguish campaign activity from true incremental impact?
4. What survey or market-testing methods are most common in this role?
5. What would excellent performance look like during the first 90 days?

## Interview Guardrails

- Do not claim VLOOKUP, Tableau, R, Python, large-dataset scale, or daily BI use.
- Say `Power BI KPI and dashboard planning`, not built production dashboards.
- Say `market-research support`, not marketing-strategy ownership.
- Say `repeatable validation checks`, not report automation ownership.
- Describe AYLA as `Data Intern`, never Client Business Analyst Intern.
