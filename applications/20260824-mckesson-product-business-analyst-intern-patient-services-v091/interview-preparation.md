## Product Discovery Approach

Use this as a proposed method:

1. Clarify the target user, patient or provider need, business outcome, constraints, and decision owner.
2. Review current process, available data, policies, known pain points, and previous research.
3. Interview representative users and stakeholders with open questions.
4. Map the current journey and process, including handoffs, delays, exceptions, and emotional pain points.
5. Separate observed problems from assumed solutions.
6. Prioritize opportunities by patient impact, feasibility, risk, effort, and strategic alignment.
7. Prototype the smallest useful solution and validate it with users.
8. Document decisions, open questions, measures, and next steps.

Do not claim previous product-discovery ownership.

## User Stories and Acceptance Criteria

### User-story structure

`As a [user], I want [capability], so that [outcome].`

Example for practice only:

`As a patient-support coordinator, I want to see incomplete enrolment requirements so that I can resolve gaps before they delay therapy access.`

### Acceptance criteria

Use `Given / When / Then` and cover:

- Happy path.
- Missing or invalid information.
- Permissions and privacy.
- Duplicate records.
- Status changes and notifications.
- Dates and boundary conditions.
- Audit trail and error messages.
- Accessibility and clear language.

State clearly that this is a method you have studied and practised, not prior formal ownership.

## Process Mapping

- Define start/end points, actors, inputs, activities, decisions, systems, handoffs, outputs, and exceptions.
- Use swimlanes when ownership crosses patient, provider, operations, product, and technology teams.
- Validate the current-state map with people who perform the work.
- Identify duplication, manual entry, unclear ownership, wait states, rework, and missing controls.
- Design a future state only after confirming root causes and constraints.

## UAT and Test Script Method

1. Trace each test to a requirement and acceptance criterion.
2. Specify preconditions, user role, data, steps, and expected result.
3. Test normal, boundary, negative, permission, privacy, and exception scenarios.
4. Record actual result, evidence, status, severity, owner, and retest outcome.
5. Reconcile data outputs and confirm the workflow matches the documented process.
6. Obtain business acceptance before release.

Present this as your proposed approach; do not claim formal UAT coordination experience.

## AI-Enabled Improvement Framework

- Start with a clear workflow problem and measurable outcome.
- Confirm that AI is appropriate compared with rules-based automation or process simplification.
- Protect patient and commercially sensitive data.
- Keep human review for consequential decisions.
- Test accuracy, bias, consistency, failure modes, and explainability.
- Log prompts, assumptions, source data, outputs, corrections, and decisions.
- Monitor whether the capability improves quality, time, or experience after deployment.

Your direct evidence is AI-assisted interpretation of validated SQL results, not production AI implementation.
