## Sourcing lifecycle

Use this structure when asked how you would support a sourcing exercise:

1. Clarify the business need, specification, quantity, timing, quality, service, compliance, and risk requirements.
2. Analyze historical demand, spend, supplier performance, market conditions, and internal constraints.
3. Identify qualified potential suppliers using approved criteria and due diligence.
4. Prepare a clear request for quotation with comparable commercial and technical requirements.
5. Normalize responses and compare price, total cost, quality, capacity, delivery, service, financial stability, location, and risk.
6. Clarify assumptions and support negotiations within approved authority and ethical rules.
7. Document the recommendation, approvals, commitments, risks, and contingency plan.
8. Monitor delivery, quality, cost, and service performance and address exceptions through defined escalation paths.

## Total cost of ownership

Explain that purchase price is only one component. Depending on the commodity, total cost may include:

- Freight, duties, tariffs, brokerage, insurance, and currency exposure.
- Tooling, qualification, testing, implementation, and engineering support.
- Inventory carrying cost, lead time, minimum orders, and working capital.
- Quality failures, inspection, rework, scrap, returns, warranty, and downtime.
- Supplier-management, transaction, service, maintenance, and end-of-life costs.
- Risk-adjusted effects of disruption, single sourcing, capacity, geography, and compliance.

State clearly that this is interview preparation, not past TCO ownership.

## Commodity-price trend analysis

1. Define the commodity, grade, unit, geography, currency, time horizon, and contract exposure.
2. Gather approved market, supplier, index, freight, currency, and internal purchase data.
3. Normalize units and currencies and separate structural trends from seasonality and one-time events.
4. Compare quoted movements with relevant cost drivers and indices.
5. Develop base, upside, and downside scenarios and quantify business exposure.
6. Recommend monitoring, contract timing, alternate sources, volume commitments, or other approved responses.
7. Document sources, assumptions, limitations, and refresh cadence.

## Supplier evaluation scorecard

Potential categories:

- Cost and commercial terms.
- Quality and defect performance.
- Delivery, lead time, capacity, and responsiveness.
- Technical capability and serviceability.
- Financial, geographic, compliance, and continuity risk.
- Sustainability, ethics, and required certifications.
- Innovation, collaboration, and improvement capability.

Weights must reflect the specific business need; do not present a generic score as objectively correct.

## Shortage-response framework

1. Confirm affected part, demand, inventory, open orders, shortage date, customer impact, and current supplier commitment.
2. Validate data and establish a single current view.
3. Contact the supplier through approved channels to understand root cause, recovery dates, and expedite options.
4. Coordinate priorities and allocation with buying, planning, operations, quality, engineering, and customer teams.
5. Evaluate approved alternatives, substitutions, transfers, rescheduling, or alternate sources.
6. Escalate based on impact and decision authority.
7. Track actions, owners, dates, risks, and customer communication through resolution.
8. Complete a post-issue review and preventive actions.

## Excel preparation

The posting explicitly mentions pivots and VLOOKUP. Pivot-table use is verified; VLOOKUP is not. Before interviewing, practise with a non-confidential sample workbook:

- Clean and standardize supplier, part, price, date, currency, and quantity fields.
- Use pivot tables for spend, supplier, commodity, site, and period summaries.
- Learn and verify VLOOKUP as well as XLOOKUP or INDEX/MATCH, if available.
- Use SUMIFS, COUNTIFS, IF/IFERROR, date functions, sorting, filtering, conditional formatting, and charts.
- Reconcile totals, check duplicates and missing keys, and document assumptions.

Do not claim VLOOKUP proficiency until you can demonstrate it independently.
