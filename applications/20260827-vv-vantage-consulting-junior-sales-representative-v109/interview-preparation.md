## Consultative Sales Conversation

Use this structure rather than rushing into a pitch:

1. **Open professionally:** Introduce yourself, establish permission to continue, and explain the purpose concisely.
2. **Discover:** Ask about the customer’s current service, usage, priorities, pain points, household or business needs, and budget boundaries.
3. **Confirm:** Summarize what you heard and correct misunderstandings before recommending anything.
4. **Recommend:** Connect only relevant product features to the stated need. Explain price, conditions, limitations, and next steps accurately.
5. **Check understanding:** Invite questions and ask whether the recommendation addresses the customer’s priorities.
6. **Resolve concerns:** Listen fully, acknowledge the concern, clarify facts, and never pressure or misrepresent.
7. **Close appropriately:** Ask for a clear next step when there is genuine fit; respect a no.
8. **Document and follow through:** Record accurate information and complete every promised action.

## Sample Discovery Questions

- What services are you currently using, and what works well or poorly?
- Which matters most to you: reliability, speed, coverage, flexibility, price, or support?
- How many people or devices regularly use the service?
- Have you experienced any recurring service or billing concerns?
- Are there features you pay for but do not use—or features you wish you had?
- What would make changing providers worthwhile for you?

Do not memorize these mechanically. Use the company’s approved script, disclosures, qualification questions, and privacy rules.

## Ethical Objection Handling

### “It costs too much.”

“I understand price is important. May I ask which part of the total cost concerns you most? I can compare the relevant features and conditions with what you currently use, but I would only recommend a change if the value fits your needs.”

### “I’m happy with my current provider.”

“That makes sense. What do you value most about your current service? If there is no meaningful gap, I respect that. If you are open to it, I can briefly explain what is different so you can make an informed comparison.”

### “I need to think about it.”

“Of course. Is there a particular feature, cost, or condition you would like clarified before deciding? I can summarize the information and the next step without pressuring you.”

### “I had a bad experience with this brand.”

“I’m sorry to hear that. I would first listen to what happened and avoid dismissing the concern. I would clarify what I am authorized to resolve, provide accurate current information, and escalate a service issue through the approved channel when needed.”

“I listen before recommending, confirm what I heard, explain information accurately, admit when I need to verify something, protect customer information, and follow through on commitments. Trust is damaged by pressure or vague promises, so I would be clear about both benefits and conditions.”

## Performance Measures to Understand

The employer may use measures such as customer contacts, qualified conversations, presentations, applications, conversions, service quality, cancellations, retention, or customer satisfaction. Do not claim familiarity with their exact measures. Ask how each metric is defined, what behaviours drive it, and how quality or cancellations affect credited sales and incentives.

**Days 31–60:** Manage customer conversations with growing independence, maintain accurate records, review personal activity and quality measures, identify common questions, and improve discovery and follow-up.

## Accuracy Boundaries

Never claim telecom knowledge, retail sales, lead generation, CRM experience, quotas, closing, upselling, customer-retention ownership, revenue results, conversion rates, or Canadian employment. Use the verified evidence: client communication, student/institution updates, accurate records, confidential data, market research, customer-behaviour analysis, Excel/SQL reporting, Agile participation, and willingness to learn.
