## Sales operations foundations

Be ready to discuss these as concepts, not past professional ownership:

- Revenue: actual and recognized revenue by period, region, territory, customer, channel, product family, and salesperson.
- Growth: year-over-year and period-over-period change, mix shifts, new versus existing customers, and comparable-period effects.
- Pipeline: opportunity count and value, stage conversion, velocity, aging, win rate, loss reasons, and forecast category.
- Customer: acquisition, retention, purchase frequency, average order value, recency, concentration, and reactivation.
- Product: sales, units, margin, mix, attach patterns, availability, returns, and lifecycle.
- Activity: calls, meetings, quotes, follow-ups, samples, opportunities created, and conversion—only where behaviour and outcome definitions are trusted.
- Data quality: completeness, validity, consistency, uniqueness, timeliness, ownership, and reconciliation.

## CRM improvement approach

1. Clarify the decisions the CRM must support and the users involved.
2. Map lead-to-order processes, roles, handoffs, required fields, stages, controls, and exceptions.
3. Profile data quality and trace common defects to process, definition, training, integration, or interface causes.
4. Agree on field definitions, ownership, validation rules, stage criteria, and exception processes.
5. Simplify workflows and dashboards around user needs; avoid collecting fields with no decision or control purpose.
6. Pilot changes, test permissions and reports, train users, collect feedback, and monitor adoption and data quality.
7. Maintain governance: owners, documentation, release control, issue logs, and recurring review.

## Forecasting answer

“I have not maintained a professional sales forecast. I would begin with agreed definitions and a reconciled historical baseline. I would compare approaches such as run rate, seasonality, pipeline-weighted forecasts, and manager judgment; test forecast accuracy by segment; investigate bias and large errors; document assumptions and overrides; and report a range or scenarios where uncertainty is meaningful. I would never present a precise forecast without explaining its drivers, data quality, and limitations.”

Useful measures:

- Forecast error and forecast bias.
- Pipeline coverage and stage conversion.
- Win rate and sales-cycle length.
- Seasonality and comparable-period adjustments.
- Committed, best-case, and upside scenarios where appropriate.

## Margin answer

“Revenue growth does not necessarily create profitable growth. I would examine gross margin dollars and percentage by customer, product, region, territory, and channel, while validating cost and discount definitions with Finance. I would also consider product mix, freight, returns, promotions, and exceptional transactions. Any pricing recommendation should account for volume response, customer value, competitive position, service cost, and approval policy.”

## Territory analysis framework

1. Define objectives: growth, coverage, service, margin, fairness, or capacity.
2. Build a common view of customers, prospects, sales, margin, activity, potential, travel, and salesperson capacity.
3. Identify concentration, whitespace, overlap, undercoverage, outliers, and capacity constraints.
4. Compare scenarios using transparent assumptions and transition risks.
5. Validate recommendations with Regional Sales Managers and monitor customer and salesperson impact after changes.

## Executive reporting structure

Lead with decisions rather than data volume:

1. Headline: what changed and why it matters.
2. Evidence: two or three KPIs with trend, target, variance, and definition.
3. Drivers: customer, product, territory, pricing, activity, or data-quality factors.
4. Risks and uncertainties: missing data, timing, unusual items, forecast assumptions.
5. Recommendation: action, owner, timing, expected effect, and follow-up measure.

## 30-60-90 day answer

### First 30 days

- Learn products, customer segments, branches, sales roles, decision cadence, CRM structure, KPI definitions, and current reports.
- Build relationships with Sales, Marketing, Finance, Procurement, and IT while clarifying ownership.
- Reconcile a small set of critical reports and document data-quality issues without changing production workflows prematurely.

### Days 31–60

- Profile CRM completeness, consistency, duplicates, aging, and stage definitions.
- Establish a prioritized issue log and propose one low-risk reporting or standardization improvement.
- Document recurring reporting logic and validate it with business owners.

### Days 61–90

- Pilot the approved improvement, measure data quality and adoption, and gather user feedback.
- Produce a repeatable management view with clear definitions, exceptions, and follow-up actions.
- Recommend a sequenced backlog for CRM, reporting, forecasting, and process improvement.
