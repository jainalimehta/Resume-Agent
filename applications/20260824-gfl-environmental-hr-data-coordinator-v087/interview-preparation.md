# Interview Preparation — GFL Environmental HR Data Coordinator

## Core Positioning

Present yourself as an early-career analytics graduate with three connected strengths: accurate administrative record handling, Excel/SQL reporting and validation, and a demonstrated interest in workforce data. Do not claim HRIS, Workday, onboarding, compliance, or training-delivery experience that you have not performed.

## 60-Second Introduction

“I recently completed a Master of Business Analytics at Edith Cowan University in Australia and now live in Toronto. At AYLA Solutions, I supported reporting requirements, SQL-based extraction and validation, advanced Excel reporting, and Agile documentation. Before that, my roles at Trans Globe Education and Arihant Investment involved accurate records, confidential data, process coordination, and client communication. I also built a published HR Analytics project covering employee, salary, performance, attendance, headcount, hiring-year, and tenure data. I am interested in this role because it combines the careful data administration I have done with the reporting and workforce-analytics skills I have developed. Workday would be new to me, but structured systems, validation, and process documentation are familiar, and I am prepared to learn it quickly.”

## Likely Questions and Strong Answer Angles

### “You have not used Workday. Why should we hire you?”

Be direct: “I have not yet used Workday, so I would not overstate that. What transfers is my experience with structured data, validation, documentation, advanced Excel, SQL, and learning business processes. I would begin by learning GFL's data definitions, approval paths, security rules, and standard operating procedures; shadow experienced users; practise in a safe environment; and use documented checklists until I can process transactions independently and accurately.”

### “How would you audit an HR data change?”

Use a controlled sequence:

1. Confirm the authorized source request and required approvals.
2. Verify employee, position, effective-date, and change fields against the source.
3. Check completeness, valid formats, duplicates, and conflicting dates.
4. Confirm the workflow reached the correct approvers and status.
5. Compare the final system record with the approved request.
6. Document the exception, avoid guessing, and escalate through the defined channel.
7. Preserve confidentiality and an audit trail throughout.

State that this is your proposed approach based on data-validation principles, not prior Workday ownership.

### “How do you prioritize multiple urgent requests?”

Explain that you would assess deadline, employee impact, compliance/payroll dependency, completeness, and processing sequence; record each request; clarify conflicts early; provide realistic updates; and never trade accuracy or confidentiality for speed.

### “How would you handle a manager who submits incomplete information?”

Confirm what is missing, explain why it is required, provide a concise checklist or example, agree on a follow-up time, and keep the request in a visible pending status. Do not alter or infer sensitive fields without authorization.

### “How do you approach an ad-hoc report?”

Clarify the business question, audience, fields, definitions, date range, confidentiality, and deadline; assess whether the data exists and access is appropriate; validate totals and exceptions; document filters and assumptions; and present the result in a usable format with limitations noted.

## Technical Knowledge to Review

- HRIS basics: employee and position records, effective-dated changes, approval workflows, role-based access, audit trails, and data retention.
- Onboarding flow: approved requisition/position, candidate-to-hire data, documentation, orientation tasks, access provisioning, payroll/benefits dependencies, completion checks, and exception follow-up.
- Data-quality dimensions: accuracy, completeness, consistency, validity, uniqueness, timeliness, and integrity.
- Excel: XLOOKUP or INDEX/MATCH, IF/IFS, COUNTIF(S), SUMIF(S), text/date cleaning, duplicate detection, conditional formatting, filters, tables, pivot tables, and protected handling of sensitive exports.
- Reporting controls: reconciliations, record counts, control totals, exception reports, versioning, peer review, documented definitions, and restricted distribution.
- Privacy mindset: minimum necessary access, secure storage and sharing, clean-desk/screen habits, careful recipients, and immediate escalation of suspected disclosure.

## Questions to Ask GFL

1. What HR transactions and data-quality issues make up most of the coordinator's first three months?
2. How are Workday training, access, and quality checks structured for a new team member?
3. Which scheduled and ad-hoc reports does this role support most often, and who uses them?
4. How do HR Operations, Talent, Payroll, and managers divide ownership across onboarding and job changes?
5. What would excellent performance look like after 30, 60, and 90 days?

## Final Interview Guardrails

- Say “Workday is new to me,” then pivot to a concrete learning and control approach.
- Never claim Canadian work experience, HRIS production use, employee onboarding ownership, or HR compliance expertise.
- Use exact titles: Data Intern, Support Executive, Administrative Executive.
- Be ready to explain every resume bullet with only the facts documented in the candidate profile.
