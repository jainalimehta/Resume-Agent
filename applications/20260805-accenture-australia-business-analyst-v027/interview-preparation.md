## Requirements-Elicitation Framework

When given a vague request:

1. Clarify the business outcome, decision, user group, pain point, urgency, and success measure.
2. Identify stakeholders, decision owners, subject-matter experts, technical contributors, and affected users.
3. Understand the current process, systems, data, handoffs, exceptions, controls, and known constraints.
4. Separate business, stakeholder, functional, data, reporting, integration, security, and non-functional needs.
5. Document assumptions, questions, dependencies, risks, priorities, and out-of-scope items.
6. Validate the requirements through walkthroughs, examples, prototypes, or process maps.
7. Establish traceability from business objective to requirement, story, design, test, defect, and approval.

Present this as a learned framework supported by related analytical evidence, not as prior end-to-end ownership.

## User Stories and Acceptance Criteria

A user story commonly follows:

> As a [user], I want [capability], so that [business value].

Example for the e-commerce project:

> As a finance analyst, I want revenue reporting to exclude pending, refunded, and cancelled activity so that management reports recognized revenue consistently.

Example acceptance criteria:

> Given an order with a pending, refunded, or cancelled status, when recognized revenue is calculated, then that order contributes zero to the recognized-revenue measure.

Good acceptance criteria are specific, testable, unambiguous, and connected to a business rule. Consider happy paths, alternate paths, invalid inputs, permissions, error handling, accessibility, performance, auditability, and data quality. Do not claim professional authorship; explain that this is how you would structure the requirement.

## Process Mapping

- Identify trigger, inputs, outputs, actors, systems, activities, decisions, handoffs, waiting, rework, exceptions, controls, and completion point.
- Validate the current-state map with people who perform the work.
- Separate symptoms from root causes and quantify impact only when evidence exists.
- Design the future state against business value, feasibility, risk, compliance, user experience, and measurement.
- Record changes to responsibilities, systems, data, controls, training, and reporting.
- Review simple flowcharts, swimlanes, SIPOC, and introductory BPMN notation.

## Risks, Issues, and Dependencies

- A **risk** is a possible future event; an **issue** is a current problem.
- A **dependency** is work, information, a decision, or a system that another deliverable relies on.
- Record description, cause, impact, probability where appropriate, owner, response, due date, status, and escalation threshold.
- Communicate with facts: impact, evidence, options, recommendation, owner, and next decision.
- Never hide uncertainty; label assumptions and confirm them.

## Mini Case Practice

Scenario: A client wants a dashboard that shows revenue, but Finance and Operations use different definitions.

Strong response structure:

1. Clarify the decision the dashboard supports and identify the metric owner.
2. Document both definitions, source systems, transaction grain, statuses, timing, refunds, cancellations, currency, and reconciliation rules.
3. Compare outputs using sample records and quantify differences only after validation.
4. Facilitate agreement on a controlled definition, exceptions, data owner, and approval route.
5. Document the requirement, calculation logic, lineage, acceptance criteria, and traceability.
6. Review feasibility with the technical team and identify dependencies or data-quality risks.
7. Validate the output with representative test cases and obtain business sign-off.
