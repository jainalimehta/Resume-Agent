# Rival Insurance Technology — Business Analyst Interview Preparation

## Positioning Statement

“I am an early-career Business Analytics graduate living in Toronto. At AYLA Solutions in Australia, I gathered and documented business and reporting requirements, supported SQL extraction and validation, prepared advanced Excel reporting, contributed dashboard insights, and participated in Agile planning and reviews. My portfolio extends that foundation through relational modelling, validation, AI-assisted interpretation of SQL results, and decision-focused recommendations. I am now looking to apply those habits in a product environment while developing deep insurance-domain knowledge.”

## Four Stories to Prepare

1. **Clarifying requirements at AYLA:** Explain the operational need, what information you gathered, how you documented it, and how the reporting output stayed aligned. Do not invent stakeholders, tools, or outcomes.
2. **Validating analytical logic:** Use the e-commerce project's recognized-revenue rule and integrity checks to explain how you test definitions, edge cases, and data quality before trusting an output.
3. **Finding patterns and recommending action:** Choose one published project, explain the SQL question and result, then describe how AI supported interpretation while the verified SQL remained the evidence base.
4. **Coordinating a workflow:** Use Trans Globe to explain how you maintained records and coordinated process updates with students and institutions.

## Honest Answers to Likely Gap Questions

**“What insurance experience do you have?”**

“I do not yet have direct insurance-software experience, so I would not overstate that. My transferable foundation is in requirements, process documentation, validation, client communication, and Agile participation. Before joining, I would build a structured view of broker workflows, personal-lines terminology, carrier connectivity, and the relevant CSIO standards, then validate that understanding with subject-matter experts.”

**“Have you performed UAT?”**

“I have not owned a formal UAT cycle. I do have hands-on validation experience: defining data rules, testing outputs, checking edge cases, and maintaining repeatable checks. I understand that UAT must trace business and functional requirements to user scenarios and acceptance criteria, record defects clearly, support retesting, and obtain business sign-off.”

**“Have you worked with APIs or CSIO?”**

“I have no verified hands-on API integration or CSIO implementation experience yet. I understand the role requires precise mapping of data and business rules across systems. My first step would be to learn the relevant CSIO standards and Rival's carrier-exchange workflows, then document field mappings, validation rules, exception paths, and acceptance criteria with technical and insurance experts.”

## Knowledge to Learn Before Interview

- The broker's role in Canadian personal-lines insurance and the high-level quote-to-bind, policy-change, renewal, billing, and claims journeys.
- High-level distinctions between habitation and auto products, without presenting yourself as a licensed insurance expert.
- What CSIO is, which standards matter to broker/carrier connectivity, and how Rival's products use them. Verify this from current primary sources before the interview.
- API basics: endpoints, request/response payloads, authentication, status codes, field mapping, validation, error handling, versioning, and auditability.
- UAT artifacts: requirement traceability, acceptance criteria, test scenarios, expected results, defect severity, retesting, and sign-off.
- SDLC stages and where a Business Analyst contributes: discovery, requirements, refinement, development support, testing, release, and feedback.

## Strong Questions for Rival

1. Which broker or carrier workflows would this Business Analyst improve first?
2. How do product, engineering, and insurance subject-matter experts refine and approve requirements?
3. What does a strong UAT cycle look like at Rival, and who owns acceptance and sign-off?
4. Which CSIO standards and carrier-integration patterns are most important to the current roadmap?
5. How does the team validate AI-supported recommendations before they influence a workflow or product decision?

## Interview Guardrails

- Say “participated in Agile planning and reviews,” not “led Agile delivery.”
- Say “AI-assisted analysis grounded in SQL outputs,” not “built predictive AI models.”
- Say “Power BI KPI and dashboard planning,” not “published Power BI dashboards.”
- Say “data validation and repeatable checks,” not “owned UAT.”
- Be direct about the insurance-domain gap, then explain a concrete learning plan.
