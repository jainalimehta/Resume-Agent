## Payroll Modernization Knowledge to Study

These are interview-study topics, not experience claims.

### Process Analysis

- As-Is process: documented current workflow, roles, systems, inputs, outputs, controls, pain points, and exceptions.
- To-Be process: proposed future workflow showing improved responsibilities, controls, integration points, and user experience.
- Gap analysis: difference between current and future states, including policy, process, data, technology, and capability gaps.
- Process measures: cycle time, exception rate, correction rate, rework, on-time completion, data accuracy, service response, and user adoption.

### Requirements

- Business requirements describe the organizational need and intended outcome.
- Functional requirements describe what the solution must do.
- Non-functional requirements describe qualities such as security, performance, accessibility, availability, auditability, and privacy.
- Data requirements define fields, sources, definitions, validation, ownership, retention, and reporting use.
- A BRD normally includes objectives, scope, stakeholders, current-state context, requirements, assumptions, constraints, dependencies, acceptance criteria, and approvals.
- Requirements traceability connects each requirement to design, configuration, test cases, defects, resolution, and acceptance.

### Payroll and Employee Data

- Typical data domains include employee identifiers, employment status, position, pay group, salary or hourly rate, earnings, deductions, benefits, taxes, time and attendance, leave, banking, costing, and organizational assignment.
- Important controls include authorization, segregation of duties, effective dates, audit trails, reconciliation, privacy, access control, and exception management.
- Data quality dimensions include completeness, accuracy, validity, consistency, uniqueness, timeliness, and referential integrity.
- Never present yourself as knowledgeable about specific payroll legislation unless you have studied and can explain it accurately.

### Data Mapping and Migration

- Source-to-target mapping identifies where each source field moves, how it is transformed, the target format, validation rule, owner, and exception handling.
- A data dictionary defines field names, business meanings, formats, allowed values, sources, ownership, and quality rules.
- Migration validation can include record counts, control totals, field-level comparisons, exception reports, duplicate checks, referential-integrity checks, and business-user sign-off.

### Testing and UAT

- Test cases should link to requirements and define prerequisites, steps, input data, expected results, actual results, status, and evidence.
- UAT confirms that configured processes and reports meet business needs in realistic scenarios.
- Payroll scenarios may include new hires, terminations, salary changes, overtime, leave, retroactive adjustments, deductions, exceptions, approvals, and reconciliation.
- Defect management includes clear reproduction steps, severity, priority, owner, target resolution, retesting, and closure evidence.

### Change and Adoption

- Stakeholder analysis identifies affected groups, influence, impact, needs, concerns, and engagement approach.
- Adoption support can include communications, role-based training, process guides, FAQs, office hours, readiness measures, and feedback channels.
- A Business Analyst helps keep requirements, process design, testing, communications, and training consistent.
