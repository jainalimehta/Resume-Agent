## Likely Interview Questions

### How would you conduct research on a transportation-maintenance issue?

Define the decision and research question; review legislation, standards, policies, complaints, claims, inspection records, work orders, weather, GPS/AVL, asset, contract, and cost data; consult operations staff; assess data quality; select quantitative and qualitative methods; establish a baseline and comparison; analyse spatial and temporal patterns; document assumptions and limitations; validate findings; develop options and recommendations; and present results with maps, tables, charts, and concise narrative.

### How do you ensure research quality?

Clarify definitions and scope, use authoritative sources, document the collection method, assess bias and missingness, validate and reconcile data, apply suitable methods, preserve reproducible steps, distinguish correlation from causation, perform reasonableness checks, disclose limitations, seek subject-matter review, and retain an auditable evidence trail.

### How would you analyse service requests and work orders?

Confirm statuses, priorities, dates, locations, assets, work types, service standards, closures, duplicates, cancellations, and reopenings. Measure volume, response time, completion time, backlog, aging, first-time completion, repeat requests, seasonal patterns, geography, crew or contractor patterns, and service-level compliance. Investigate data quality before drawing conclusions.

### How would you identify a problematic maintenance area?

Combine asset condition, inspection findings, service requests, work orders, claims, rehabilitation history, traffic or usage, weather, and location data. Normalize for exposure where possible, map clusters, examine trends and repeat events, validate with field staff, distinguish data artifacts from operational problems, and recommend prioritized investigation or treatment.

### Tell us about writing a research summary.

Use the GST project. Explain the research objective, survey method, SPSS analysis, significant patterns or barriers at a non-fabricated level, limitations, recommendations, and A grade. Never invent sample size or statistical significance.

### How would you communicate with operations staff and the public?

Lead with the question and operational impact, use plain language, separate confirmed facts from assumptions, show concise evidence, listen to field context, document actions and owners, protect confidential or claims information, and follow established escalation and records procedures.

### How do you prioritize competing research requests?

Assess safety, legal or claims exposure, service impact, statutory or Council deadline, urgency, data availability, effort, dependencies, and decision value. Confirm priorities with management, maintain a visible work plan, communicate trade-offs early, and document changes.

### TMMS/EWMS Maximo

- Core concepts: assets, locations, hierarchies, service requests, work orders, priorities, statuses, crews, labour, materials, inspections, failure codes, job plans, preventive maintenance, attachments, costs, and history.

### GPS/AVL

- Typical data: vehicle/device ID, timestamp, latitude/longitude, speed, heading, ignition, route, activity, and event status.
- Common preparation: time-zone handling, duplicate pings, missing intervals, invalid coordinates, map matching, trip/stop definitions, outliers, device changes, and privacy controls.
- Possible analysis: route coverage, treatment evidence, response patterns, idle time, material application, travel time, geofenced activity, and service-level support. Treat these as study examples, not verified City use.

### GIS

- Vector points, lines, and polygons; layers; attributes; coordinate reference systems and projections; geocoding; spatial joins; buffering; intersection; proximity; topology; symbology; and map layouts.
- Transportation examples: roads, sidewalks, signs, bridges, work orders, inspections, claims, 311 requests, yards, routes, wards, and maintenance districts.
- Data-quality topics: geometry validity, duplicate features, missing locations, inaccurate coordinates, incompatible projections, outdated layers, and inconsistent asset identifiers.
