## Business Systems Analyst Framework

When given a new feature or system problem:

1. Clarify the business outcome, affected users, current process, pain points, scope, constraints, and decision owner.
2. Identify business, functional, data, reporting, integration, security, and non-functional requirements.
3. Map the current process and proposed future process, including handoffs, decisions, exceptions, and controls.
4. Convert approved needs into structured requirements or user stories with acceptance criteria.
5. Record assumptions, dependencies, risks, priorities, owners, and traceability.
6. Review with business and technical stakeholders and reconcile conflicts.
7. Support test design, defect clarification, UAT, readiness, documentation, and post-release learning.

Present this as a learned approach supported by related evidence—not as prior end-to-end ownership.

## User Stories and Acceptance Criteria

A clear user story commonly follows:

> As a [user], I want [capability], so that [business value].

Good acceptance criteria should be specific, testable, unambiguous, and connected to the business rule. Practise Given/When/Then:

> Given an authenticated customer with a completed order, when the customer opens order history, then the system displays the order date, recognized total, payment status, and line items.

Be ready to discuss:

- Happy paths, alternate paths, errors, permissions, data validation, performance, accessibility, and audit needs.
- Definition of Ready versus Definition of Done.
- Requirement traceability from need to story, design, test, defect, and approval.

Do not claim professional user-story or acceptance-criteria authorship.

## Process Mapping and Improvement

- Identify trigger, inputs, outputs, roles, systems, decisions, handoffs, waiting, rework, controls, and exceptions.
- Validate the map with the people who perform the work.
- Separate symptoms from root causes using evidence and repeated questioning.
- Evaluate improvements against customer value, risk, effort, feasibility, cost, and measurement.
- Document future-state responsibilities, controls, training needs, and KPIs.

Possible notation to review: simple flowcharts, swimlanes, SIPOC, and introductory BPMN concepts.

## Testing and UAT Preparation

Jainali has verified SQL/data validation, not formal professional UAT. Suggested approach:

1. Trace scenarios to approved requirements and acceptance criteria.
2. Define preconditions, test data, steps, expected result, actual result, and evidence.
3. Cover normal, boundary, invalid, duplicate, permission, integration, and recovery cases.
4. Log defects with reproducible steps, environment, severity, priority, owner, and status.
5. Retest fixes and conduct regression checks on related functionality.
6. Summarize pass status, open risks, workarounds, and readiness for business approval.
