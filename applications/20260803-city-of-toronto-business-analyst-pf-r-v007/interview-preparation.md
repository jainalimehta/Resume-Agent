## Likely Interview Questions

### How would you approach an IBM Maximo project without prior Maximo experience?

Be direct about the gap. Explain that you would learn Parks & Recreation's asset hierarchy, locations, work types, preventive and corrective maintenance, service requests, work orders, labour/materials, scheduling, mobile use, GIS/spatial integration, security roles, and reporting. You would partner with asset-management SMEs and technical resources, document current and future processes, define requirements and acceptance criteria, maintain traceability, support configuration reviews and UAT, and validate that the solution meets operational needs.

### How do you gather and validate requirements?

Use AYLA as the verified example. Explain your method: identify stakeholders and decisions; review existing documents and data; ask about goals, users, rules, exceptions, pain points, inputs, outputs, controls, and integrations; document requirements clearly; distinguish needs from proposed solutions; define acceptance criteria; resolve conflicts using evidence and agreed objectives; review and obtain confirmation; maintain traceability through design and testing.

### How would you facilitate an As-Is/To-Be workshop?

Present this as your approach, not previous workshop leadership. Prepare objectives, participants, scope, source documents, and a draft context map. Establish shared terminology, map the current process with roles, events, activities, decisions, data, systems, handoffs, waits, exceptions, and controls. Validate pain points and root causes, then design future-state options, assess impacts and feasibility, confirm decisions, owners, open questions, and next steps.

### What is BPMN?

Know the basics: events, activities/tasks, gateways, sequence flows, message flows, pools, lanes, subprocesses, and artifacts. Explain that BPMN provides a consistent visual language for processes and cross-role handoffs. Be honest that your verified experience is process mapping rather than production use of a named BPMN tool.

### How would you identify root causes and improvement opportunities?

Define the problem with evidence, establish a baseline, observe the process, review data and exceptions, use techniques such as Five Whys, cause-and-effect analysis, Pareto analysis, and value-stream thinking, distinguish symptoms from causes, develop options, assess impacts and risks, pilot where appropriate, and measure agreed KPIs after implementation.

### How would you develop performance indicators?

Start from the service objective and intended decision. Define each KPI's business meaning, formula, data source, owner, frequency, target, segmentation, exclusions, and limitations. Validate data quality, establish a baseline, avoid vanity metrics, and pair efficiency indicators with quality, accessibility, and user-outcome measures.

### How would you create and execute a test plan?

State this as your working method, not verified ownership: link scenarios to approved requirements; cover standard, boundary, exception, integration, role/security, data-conversion, and reporting cases; define preconditions, test data, steps, expected results, owners, and pass/fail criteria; log defects with evidence and severity; retest; track coverage; and obtain business sign-off.

### How would you manage conflicting requirements?

Clarify the underlying goals, constraints, affected users, regulatory or policy requirements, and decision authority. Document the conflict and options, use process and data evidence, assess impacts, facilitate trade-offs, escalate when governance requires it, record the decision and rationale, and update traceability.

### Tell us about financial analysis.

Use the e-commerce project: recognized-revenue rules, monthly revenue, customer spend, order frequency, product performance, and payment methods. Explain how definition quality and exclusions affect reported indicators. Do not claim a municipal budget or cost-benefit study.
