## What the Development Team Does

Vital invests in, operates, and develops healthcare infrastructure. A development team may coordinate a project through stages such as:

1. **Opportunity and feasibility:** property/site review, market need, initial concept, financial and operational feasibility.
2. **Due diligence:** title, environmental, planning, servicing, building condition, legal, financial, and other investigations.
3. **Concept and design:** consultant engagement, requirements, design development, estimates, and stakeholder review.
4. **Planning and municipal approvals:** pre-consultation, application materials, submissions, comments, revisions, fees, conditions, and approvals.
5. **Procurement:** RFPs, proposals, consultant/contractor selection, agreements, and purchase commitments.
6. **Construction and delivery:** schedules, budgets, change management, invoices, site documentation, risks, and reporting.
7. **Commissioning and close-out:** inspections, deficiencies, occupancy/turnover, final documentation, warranties, reconciliation, and transition to operations.

The Coordinator supports the information flow and administrative controls across these stages; the role does not independently make engineering, planning, legal, or investment decisions.

## Municipal Planning Basics

Exact processes differ by municipality and project. For Toronto examples:

- The Official Plan sets broad goals and policies for physical change.
- The Zoning By-law controls permitted uses and development standards such as height, setbacks, parking, and loading.
- A Zoning By-law Amendment may be required when a proposal does not comply with existing zoning.
- Site Plan Control reviews design and technical site matters such as access, servicing, waste, parking/loading, landscaping, accessibility, and exterior design.
- Other applications can include Official Plan Amendments, subdivisions, condominiums, and minor variances.
- Toronto uses an Application Submission Tool for applications, pre-application consultation requests, updated materials, and status tracking.
- Submission requirements can include forms, fee schedules, project data, a covering memo, checklists, plans, studies, and other supporting materials.

A coordinator's role is to maintain the requirement checklist, confirm responsibilities and dates, organize the correct version of every document, track fees/submission status/comments, and follow up with the accountable professional. Never interpret planning law independently when the matter requires a planner or legal adviser.

## Invoice-Control Workflow

1. Receive and date-stamp/log the invoice.
2. Confirm vendor, project, invoice number, dates, amount, taxes, and duplicate status.
3. Match to the agreement, proposal, purchase order, budget/cost code, or other required support.
4. Confirm the work/service period and obtain the responsible project lead's approval.
5. Resolve discrepancies with the appropriate vendor or internal owner; document the communication.
6. Submit to Finance using the correct process and coding.
7. Update the tracker and retain all supporting documentation.
8. Follow up on overdue approval/payment and close the item only when status is confirmed.

Red flags include duplicate invoices, missing approval, incorrect project/cost code, amounts exceeding authorization, unsupported expenses, tax errors, changed banking details, and invoice work that does not match the agreement.

## RFP Coordination Workflow

1. Confirm scope, requirements, timeline, evaluation approach, and authorized distribution list.
2. Assemble and quality-check the package.
3. Issue consistently to invited parties and log distribution.
4. Centralize questions and distribute approved responses/addenda fairly.
5. Securely log submissions and check administrative completeness.
6. Circulate to authorized reviewers and maintain the review schedule.
7. Track clarifications, evaluation, decision, notifications, and agreement follow-up.
8. Archive the final record and preserve confidentiality.

The coordinator administers the process; technical and commercial selection decisions belong to authorized reviewers.

## Practice Scenario 1: Invoice Does Not Match the Proposal

**Prompt:** A consultant invoice exceeds the amount expected and the VP is unavailable.

Strong answer:

- Do not alter, approve, or reject it without authority.
- Log the invoice and compare it with the agreement/proposal, prior invoices, approved changes, and cost code.
- Identify the precise discrepancy and collect supporting evidence.
- Ask the consultant for clarification if permitted, copying the appropriate internal owner.
- Route it to the designated alternate approver or hold it according to procedure.
- Update the tracker with the issue, owner, and next follow-up date.
- Keep Finance informed if the payment timeline may be affected.

## Practice Scenario 2: Municipal Submission Deadline at Risk

**Prompt:** Two consultant documents are missing the day before submission.

- Confirm which items are missing, whether they are mandatory, and the exact deadline.
- Contact the responsible consultants immediately with a clear request and time required.
- Inform the project lead of the risk, current action, and options.
- Check whether other package components can be completed and quality-checked in parallel.
- Do not submit an incomplete package without authorized direction.
- Record follow-ups, decisions, and revised timing.
- After resolution, identify why the dependency was missed and improve the checklist or reminder cadence.

## Practice Scenario 3: Competing Priorities

**Prompt:** You need to finalize minutes, process urgent invoices, and prepare an RFP package on the same afternoon.

- Clarify hard deadlines, business impact, dependencies, and approval cut-offs.
- Break each item into required steps and estimate effort.
- Confirm priorities with the VP/project leads when conflicts remain.
- Complete quick dependency-releasing actions first, such as routing invoices for approval or requesting missing RFP material.
- Use a visible tracker and communicate revised completion times early.
- Protect quality; do not silently rush and create document or payment errors.
