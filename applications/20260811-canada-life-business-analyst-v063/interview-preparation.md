## Requirements-Gathering Framework

1. Clarify the business objective, problem, expected outcome, sponsor, users, and decision required.
2. Understand the current process, pain points, controls, systems, data, handoffs, exceptions, and stakeholders.
3. Elicit needs through interviews, workshops, document review, observation, and data analysis.
4. Distinguish business requirements from solution preferences and document assumptions and constraints.
5. Make requirements specific, testable, traceable, feasible, and unambiguous.
6. Validate requirements through walkthroughs, examples, prototypes, process maps, and acceptance criteria.
7. Prioritize using business value, risk, urgency, dependencies, effort, and regulatory need.
8. Baseline approved requirements and control changes through impact assessment and documented decisions.

## Process-Analysis Framework

- Map the current state with actors, steps, systems, decisions, inputs, outputs, controls, wait times, and exceptions.
- Identify rework, duplicate entry, unclear ownership, manual handoffs, delays, inconsistent rules, and control gaps.
- Confirm root causes with stakeholders and data before recommending a change.
- Design a future state that addresses the cause, preserves required controls, and defines ownership and measures.
- Compare expected benefit, effort, risk, customer effect, change impact, and implementation dependencies.

Present this as your method; do not claim professional process-redesign ownership beyond verified work.

## Testing and Traceability

1. Maintain a requirements traceability matrix linking requirement, process, solution element, test case, owner, and result.
2. Cover positive, negative, boundary, exception, role/permission, data-quality, integration, and regression scenarios.
3. Define expected results and test data before execution.
4. Record defects with steps, evidence, expected versus actual result, severity, owner, and status.
5. Retest fixes and relevant regression areas.
6. Obtain documented business acceptance before implementation.

Your verified experience is repeatable data validation and GitHub Actions checks—not formal system test strategy or UAT coordination.
