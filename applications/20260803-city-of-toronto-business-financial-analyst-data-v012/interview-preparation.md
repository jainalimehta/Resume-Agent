### How would you support data migration?

1. Confirm business scope, owners, source systems, target objects, definitions, controls, and acceptance criteria.
2. Profile source data for completeness, validity, consistency, uniqueness, conformity, and referential integrity.
3. Maintain source-to-target mappings, transformation rules, defaults, exceptions, dependencies, and unresolved decisions.
4. Cleanse and standardize data with business-owner approval and an auditable issue log.
5. Run mock loads, validate technical success, reconcile control totals, and obtain business sign-off.
6. Track defects, repeat testing, plan cutover sequencing, preserve lineage, and complete post-load validation.

State that this is your studied approach, not prior production ownership.

### How would you prevent data inaccuracies in the future state?

Use clear data ownership, mandatory fields, controlled values, validation rules, uniqueness and referential-integrity controls, standardized definitions, role-based access, approval workflows, exception reports, reconciliations, monitoring KPIs, training, and periodic data-quality reviews. Document each control and its owner.

### How would you test a future-state solution?

Trace requirements to test conditions and cases; define expected results, test data, environment, owner, and evidence; execute positive, negative, boundary, role/access, interface, and reconciliation scenarios; log defects with severity and reproducible steps; retest fixes; run regression; report coverage and blockers; obtain exit approval; support cutover rehearsal and post-production validation.

Use your SQL validation and GitHub Actions work as evidence of a testing mindset, not as enterprise UAT experience.

### Give an example of a business rule you documented.

Use the e-commerce recognized-revenue rule: pending, refunded, and cancelled activity was excluded from recognized revenue. Explain why a shared definition matters, how it affects reports, and how validation protects consistent use. Do not invent a financial impact.

### How would you reconcile migrated financial or material data?

Compare source and target record counts and control totals; reconcile quantities, values, statuses, units of measure, currencies, dates, organizational assignments, and key relationships; investigate unmatched, duplicate, rejected, or transformed records; document tolerances and exceptions; rerun after correction; obtain data-owner approval.

### How do you communicate issues, risks, and decisions?

Maintain separate logs with identifier, description, impact, likelihood/severity, owner, due date, status, mitigation or resolution, dependencies, and decision required. Escalate early using concise evidence, options, recommendation, and schedule/business impact. Protect sensitive information and record approvals.

### How would you prepare an executive decision document?

Use: decision required, executive summary, background, current state, options, evaluation criteria, cost/benefit, risks, dependencies, stakeholder impacts, recommendation, implementation implications, and next steps. Separate facts, assumptions, and estimates.

### How do you manage competing deadlines?

Prioritize by milestone criticality, dependency, financial/operational impact, risk, effort, and decision deadline; confirm priorities with the manager; break work into visible deliverables; track owners and due dates; communicate trade-offs early; protect quality through checklists and peer review.

## SAP and Materials-Management Study Guide

### SAP ECC and S/4HANA

- ECC is the older SAP ERP generation; S/4HANA uses the HANA platform and a simplified data model.
- Learn the difference between master data, transactional data, configuration, custom code, interfaces, reports, and historical/archive data.
- Typical transformation work includes discovery, process design, data assessment, fit-to-standard analysis, configuration, development, migration cycles, testing, training, cutover, hypercare, and sustainment.
- Do not imply that every S/4HANA program follows the same implementation method; learn the City's actual program scope.

### SAP MM

- Materials Management commonly covers material master data, vendors/business partners, purchasing, purchase requisitions, purchase orders, goods receipt, invoice verification, inventory, valuation, and organizational structures.
- Key data-quality themes include duplicate materials/vendors, descriptions, units of measure, purchasing groups, plants/storage locations, valuation classes, open transactions, and obsolete records.

### LE-WM and EWM

- LE-WM is SAP's classic warehouse-management capability; EWM provides more advanced warehouse execution and control.
- Study storage types/bins, stock placement and removal, goods movements, handling units, warehouse tasks/orders, physical inventory, and integration with procurement and finance.
- Do not claim configuration knowledge or implementation experience.

## Testing Types to Know

- **Unit testing:** confirms an individual configuration or development component.
- **Functional testing:** confirms a business function against requirements.
- **Integration testing:** confirms end-to-end processes and interfaces across systems/modules.
- **Regression testing:** confirms changes did not break previously working functions.
- **Parallel testing:** compares legacy and future-state outputs during the same period or dataset.
- **User acceptance testing:** business users confirm fitness for use and requirement satisfaction.
- **Cutover testing/rehearsal:** validates sequence, timing, dependencies, roles, data loads, and fallback readiness.
- **Post-load validation:** confirms completeness, accuracy, balances, relationships, access, and usability after migration.
