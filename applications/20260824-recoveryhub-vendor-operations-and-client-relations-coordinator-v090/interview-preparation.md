## Handling a Difficult Vendor Conversation

Use this proposed framework:

1. Prepare the facts: case, timeline, service expectation, evidence, and desired outcome.
2. Open calmly and state the purpose without accusation.
3. Let the vendor explain; listen for facts, constraints, and emotion.
4. Acknowledge the concern without agreeing to unsupported claims.
5. Restate the standard or directive and explain the operational impact.
6. Agree on a specific next action, owner, and time.
7. Document the conversation and confirm it in writing.
8. Escalate promptly if legal, safety, compliance, financial, reputational, or repeated-performance risk exceeds your authority.

State that this is your approach, not a claim of past bailiff-conflict ownership.

## Smart Escalation Framework

Escalate when one or more of these apply:

- Safety, legal, regulatory, privacy, fraud, or reputational risk.
- Material financial exposure or potential client harm.
- Contract ambiguity or a request outside delegated authority.
- A repeated SLA failure after documented coaching or follow-up.
- Conflicting facts that cannot be verified independently.
- A vendor refuses a directive, becomes threatening, or requests an exception.
- A decision could set a precedent across the network.

When escalating, provide the facts, evidence, risk, actions already taken, time sensitivity, available options, and your recommendation.

## Vendor Scorecard Knowledge

### Example scorecard categories

- Assignment acceptance rate.
- Time to first action and time to completion.
- SLA compliance rate.
- Documentation completeness and accuracy.
- Client or internal complaint rate.
- Rework, exception, and escalation frequency.
- Communication responsiveness.
- Compliance-review status.
- Recovery or remarketing outcome measures defined by RecoveryHub.

### How to build it

1. Clarify the business objective and vendor behaviours the scorecard should influence.
2. Define each metric, numerator, denominator, exclusions, source, owner, and reporting period.
3. Validate data completeness and reconcile totals.
4. Set targets and thresholds only with authorized business owners.
5. Use trends and peer comparisons carefully; account for case mix and territory differences.
6. Pair scores with comments, exceptions, and agreed actions.
7. Review regularly and document disputes or corrections.

Do not claim these were implemented in your portfolio.

## Contract Review Basics

Review for learning purposes under leadership, focusing on:

- Parties, scope of services, territory, term, renewal, and termination.
- Fees, invoicing, taxes, expenses, and payment timing.
- Service levels, reporting, documentation, and audit rights.
- Insurance, licences, subcontracting, and compliance obligations.
- Confidentiality, privacy, data security, and record retention.
- Indemnity, liability limits, dispute resolution, and governing law.
- Change control, assignment, force majeure, and notice requirements.

Never offer legal conclusions. Flag unclear, inconsistent, missing, or commercially unusual terms for authorized reviewers.
