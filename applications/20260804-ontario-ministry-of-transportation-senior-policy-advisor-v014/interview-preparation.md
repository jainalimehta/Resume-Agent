## Policy-Development Framework

1. **Define the issue:** mandate, problem statement, affected users, urgency, current state, and decision required.
2. **Establish outcomes:** mobility, access, safety, equity, economic productivity, affordability, sustainability, reliability, and implementation feasibility.
3. **Build evidence:** administrative data, travel-demand data, demographics, land use, infrastructure, costs, user research, literature, jurisdictional scans, and stakeholder input.
4. **Develop options:** include status quo; specify policy, legislative, regulatory, program, funding, information, partnership, pricing, or operational tools.
5. **Assess impacts:** economic, social, fiscal, environmental, legal, Indigenous, municipal, regional, equity, accessibility, privacy, implementation, and communications considerations.
6. **Consult and refine:** map stakeholders, test assumptions, document disagreements, adjust options, and preserve a transparent engagement record.
7. **Recommend:** compare options against explicit criteria, explain trade-offs, identify risks and mitigations, and state the preferred path.
8. **Implement and evaluate:** governance, accountabilities, work plan, resources, milestones, communications, performance measures, data plan, review points, and corrective action.

State that this is a studied framework, not prior ownership of an Ontario policy file.

### How would you develop a policy to reduce congestion?

Define the corridor/system problem and desired outcomes; segment users, trip purposes, times, and modes; assess demand, capacity, reliability, incidents, land use, population/employment growth, freight, equity, and environmental effects; consult ministries, agencies, municipalities, Indigenous communities, operators, businesses, and users; compare infrastructure, transit, active transportation, demand-management, pricing, technology, land-use, and operational options; assess impacts and implementation; recommend a portfolio with measures and review points.

Do not advocate a specific solution without evidence or authority.

### How would you conduct an environmental and jurisdictional scan?

Define the policy question and comparison criteria; use authoritative legislation, strategies, evaluations, budgets, data, and academic evidence; select comparable jurisdictions; document context and limitations; compare tools, governance, funding, outcomes, equity, and implementation; validate with experts; identify transferable lessons rather than copying another jurisdiction.

### How would you assess economic, social, and fiscal impacts?

- Economic: travel time/reliability, labour-market access, goods movement, productivity, business costs, development, and distributional effects.
- Social: accessibility, affordability, safety, health, community connectivity, rural/northern access, and impacts on different populations.
- Fiscal: capital/operating costs, revenues, lifecycle costs, funding sources, affordability, risk, and opportunity cost.
- Document assumptions, ranges, sensitivity, uncertainty, and who bears costs or receives benefits.

### How would you build consensus among conflicting stakeholders?

Map interests, authority, impacts, and non-negotiables; establish shared outcomes and decision rules; provide common evidence; use accessible materials; hear concerns separately and collectively; identify areas of agreement; develop options and mitigations; document unresolved issues; avoid promising outcomes outside authority; and escalate decisions transparently.

### How would you brief senior management?

Lead with the decision required and recommendation. Present issue, context, evidence, stakeholder positions, options, impacts, risks, implementation, communications, and next steps. Separate facts from assumptions and clearly state uncertainty. Prepare concise speaking points and anticipated questions.

### Tell us about leading a project.

Do not claim project leadership. Use AYLA or GST research to demonstrate planning and coordination at your actual level: clarify objective, break work into tasks, manage data and documentation, communicate progress, validate outputs, and complete findings. Then explain the additional governance, delegation, risk, and stakeholder practices you would apply under leadership.

### How do you provide advice when evidence is incomplete?

Clarify the decision deadline; identify knowns, unknowns, and critical assumptions; assess risk of delay versus action; seek targeted evidence and expert input; develop provisional options; use scenarios/sensitivity analysis; recommend a reversible or staged approach where appropriate; define monitoring and review triggers; disclose uncertainty plainly.

## Transportation-System Knowledge to Build

### Multi-Modal System

- Roads/highways, municipal streets, public transit, intercity bus/rail, freight rail, marine, aviation, cycling, walking, and emerging mobility.
- Consider movement of both people and goods, connections between modes, first/last mile, network resilience, and urban/rural/northern differences.

### Transportation and Land Use

- Density, mix of uses, jobs/housing distribution, development timing, parking, street networks, station access, and transit-supportive design affect travel demand and mode choice.
- Transportation investment also changes accessibility, development pressure, land value, and infrastructure needs.

### System Optimization

- Make better use of existing capacity through operations, incident management, traveller information, transit priority, signal coordination, demand management, pricing, freight strategies, asset management, and technology.
- Evaluate network effects and unintended consequences rather than optimizing one location in isolation.

### Equity and Accessibility

- Assess who benefits, who pays, who faces barriers, geographic distribution, affordability, disability access, service availability, safety, and access to jobs/services.
- Use disaggregated data and direct engagement while respecting privacy and limitations.
