## What the Role Is Designed to Achieve

The Project Coordinator is the operational link between Directors, Project Leads, Technical Teams, Finance, Marketing, ICT, other coordinators, and external contacts. Success means:

- projects, clients, contacts, stages, budgets, and resources are set up consistently;
- required time, expense, and project information is complete and current;
- reports and dashboards reconcile to reliable source data;
- Finance receives usable information for billing and invoicing;
- pipeline information moves cleanly from Marketing into Polaris;
- staff receive prompt system support and clear training;
- issues and enhancement requests are documented, tested, and followed through; and
- the Toronto office operates professionally and safely.

## Polaris and ERP Fundamentals

The posting describes Polaris as WT’s Salesforce-based ERP platform. Do not claim knowledge of WT’s configuration. Explain the concepts:

- **CRM data:** clients, contacts, leads, opportunities, interactions, and pipeline stages.
- **Project data:** project ID, project name, client, lead/director, stage, dates, status, budget, resources, and billing details.
- **Time and expense data:** staff time, expense category, project code, dates, approval status, and supporting records.
- **ERP controls:** required fields, standardized values, role-based access, approvals, audit history, data ownership, and reconciliation.
- **Source of truth:** the approved system and current record used for operational reporting—not a personal spreadsheet or verbal update.

Strong interview line: “Before changing data or a workflow, I would learn the approved field definitions, status values, ownership, approval authority, and downstream reporting or billing impact.”

## Project Lifecycle to Understand

1. **Lead/opportunity:** Marketing records the prospective client, opportunity, owner, value or scope information, and current stage.
2. **Project creation:** An authorized opportunity becomes a project with identifiers, client/contact relationships, stage, budget, resources, dates, and project leadership.
3. **Delivery administration:** Timecards, expenses, changes, status, forecasts, and required details are kept complete and current.
4. **Billing preparation:** Project and financial inputs are reviewed for completeness and exceptions before Finance issues invoices through the approved process.
5. **Reporting:** Dashboards summarize pipeline, project status, budgets, time, expenses, billing, and data-quality exceptions using agreed definitions.
6. **Close-out:** Required billing, project status, documentation, and closure fields are completed and reconciled.

This is a conceptual lifecycle. WT’s actual gates and approvals must come from its procedures and Polaris configuration.

## Data-Quality Controls to Discuss

- use stable project, client, and contact identifiers;
- standardize stage, status, service, office, and owner values;
- validate mandatory fields before advancing a record;
- check dates, budgets, resources, timecards, expenses, and billing fields for completeness;
- detect duplicates and conflicting client/contact records;
- reconcile dashboard totals to source records;
- record who owns each exception and when it is due;
- avoid silent corrections when an approval or audit trail is required; and
- document definitions, update cadence, and system-of-record rules.

## Reporting and Dashboard Ideas

Subject to WT’s approved metrics:

- projects by stage, service, office, lead, and status;
- opportunities by pipeline stage and aging;
- missing or stale project data;
- timecard and expense completion exceptions;
- budget versus recorded time/expense information;
- billing readiness and unresolved billing exceptions;
- invoice status and aging;
- outstanding actions by owner;
- support issues by category, age, and resolution status; and
- data-quality trend by reporting period.

Each metric requires a definition, source, refresh time, and owner. Power BI should only be used after the underlying data and business definitions are reliable.

## System Support, Testing, and Bug Reporting

When a staff member reports an issue:

1. Confirm the user, role, record, process step, time, and expected versus actual result.
2. Check whether it is a training question, data issue, access issue, configuration issue, or reproducible defect.
3. Preserve screenshots or error text without exposing confidential information.
4. Reproduce safely in an approved test environment when available.
5. Record severity, impact, affected users, workaround, owner, and status.
6. Escalate to the correct ICT, Australian, or platform stakeholder.
7. Test the correction against defined acceptance criteria.
8. Communicate the resolution and update training or process documentation if needed.

Do not claim production testing experience. Relate this method to Jainali’s verified validation, GitHub Actions, requirements, documentation, and Agile exposure.
