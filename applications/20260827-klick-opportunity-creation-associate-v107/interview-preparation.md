## Research Workflow

Use this seven-step answer:

1. Clarify the question: target profile, market, geography, therapeutic area, decision, deadline, and intended audience.
2. Start with primary sources: company websites, filings, investor materials, trial registries, regulator information, press releases, and official leadership biographies.
3. Add reputable secondary sources for context and competitive interpretation.
4. Capture source URL, publication date, event date, and retrieval date.
5. Separate facts, estimates, and your inference.
6. Validate high-impact claims through a second reliable source where possible.
7. Summarize the opportunity, evidence, relevance to Klick, uncertainties, and recommended next action.

Strong phrase:

> I would optimize for decision usefulness and traceability. A senior growth leader should be able to see what happened, why it matters, how confident we are, and where the evidence came from.

## CRM Foundations

Salesforce's official learning material identifies Leads, Accounts, Contacts, and Opportunities as core Sales Cloud objects. Leads represent early prospects, Accounts represent companies, Contacts represent people, and Opportunities represent qualified deals being tracked. Source: https://trailhead.salesforce.com/content/learn/modules/sales-cloud-configuration-basics/customize-sales-cloud

Do not claim hands-on Salesforce experience. Be prepared to explain these concepts:

- Lead: unqualified or early-stage potential prospect.
- Account: organization or company record.
- Contact: person related to an account.
- Opportunity: qualified potential deal with stage, expected timing, and other pipeline details.
- Activity: call, email, meeting, task, or approved interaction history.
- Campaign: organized marketing initiative and its members/responses, depending on configuration.

Every company customizes fields, stages, required data, qualification criteria, automations, and permissions. Learn Klick's definitions instead of assuming Salesforce defaults.

## CRM Data-Quality Checklist

- Search before creating to prevent duplicates.
- Use standardized company names and domains.
- Verify contact name, title, organization, and current employment.
- Record lead source and supporting evidence.
- Populate required fields and use approved picklist values.
- Add activity notes promptly and objectively.
- Confirm owner, status, stage, next action, and due date.
- Avoid unsupported opportunity values or probabilities.
- Preserve consent, privacy, confidentiality, and access requirements.
- Merge or deactivate records only under approved procedures.

If you discover conflicting data, retain the evidence, identify the likely source of truth, and route the correction through the defined owner rather than silently overwriting material history.

## Lead Triage Framework

First determine whether the request is complete and in scope. Then evaluate:

- Strategic fit with Klick's capabilities and markets
- Organization and product relevance
- Commercial or pipeline timing
- Evidence of need or intent
- Existing relationship and account ownership
- Urgency and requested response time
- Potential value and strategic importance
- Conflict, compliance, privacy, or reputational concerns
- Information completeness and confidence

Example triage note:

> Inbound inquiry received from [organization/contact] regarding [need]. Evidence and relationship history are [summary]. Recommended owner is [team/role] because [reason]. Response requested by [date]. Open questions are [items].

Do not invent a scoring model. Ask Klick how it defines a viable lead and routes priorities.

## Timely Handoff

A good handoff contains:

- Request or opportunity summary
- Prospect/account/contact identifiers
- Source and timestamp
- Relevant history and prior interactions
- Research/backgrounder link
- Stated need, urgency, and deadline
- Recommended owner and reason
- Next action and due date
- Risks, gaps, and unresolved questions

Confirm receipt when urgency or ambiguity makes it necessary. A handoff is not complete merely because an email was sent.

## Responsible AI Discussion

Your safe evidence is AI-assisted analysis based on validated SQL outputs and Machine Learning coursework. It is not AI-governance expertise.

A credible answer:

> I see AI as an accelerator for structured work, not an authority. For prospect research, it could help organize public information, generate research questions, or draft a first-pass summary. I would protect confidential information, use only approved tools, retain sources, verify every material claim, watch for hallucination and bias, and ensure a human owner approves client-facing content.

NIST's voluntary AI Risk Management Framework organizes risk management around Govern, Map, Measure, and Manage and emphasizes trustworthiness considerations. Review: https://www.nist.gov/itl/ai-risk-management-framework

Healthcare-oriented controls to mention:

- Approved tools and data classifications
- No confidential, personal, health, client, or proprietary data in unapproved systems
- Source attribution and claim verification
- Human review and accountability
- Bias and harmful-output checks
- Version/model awareness and reproducibility where required
- Clear separation of generated content from verified facts
- Logging, escalation, and correction of material errors

## Likely Interview Questions

### Why Klick?

Connect Klick's integrated life-sciences capabilities, evidence-based culture, AI orientation, collaborative Growth team, and the role's combination of research and execution. Refer to what you learned from Klick's current offerings instead of repeating award lists.

### Why opportunity creation?

Explain that you enjoy turning fragmented information into structured insight and coordinated action. Connect market research, client service, analytical reporting, records, and handoffs.

### You have not used Salesforce. How will you succeed?

> I would not claim platform proficiency. My transferable base is accurate record maintenance, data validation, relational-data concepts, client updates, and structured documentation. Before independent work, I would learn Klick's object model, field definitions, qualification stages, permissions, naming rules, and duplicate-management process; complete approved Salesforce learning; practise in a safe environment; and have early records reviewed.

### How do you manage multiple priorities?

Assess urgency, strategic impact, deadline, dependency, owner, completeness, and effort. Keep one controlled queue, clarify priorities when requests conflict, communicate expected completion, and escalate blockers before a deadline is missed.

### What makes a strong executive briefing?

Lead with the decision or opportunity, then the evidence, relevance, risk/uncertainty, and recommended action. Put methodology and source detail in an appendix or linked backgrounder.

### How do you handle an inaccurate CRM record?

Verify the record and source, check duplicate/history/ownership, preserve material context, follow the approved correction process, notify affected owners when necessary, and document the change.
