## PMIS Conceptual Model

A PMIS should provide a governed source of project information across scope, schedule, cost, risk, contracts, documents, decisions, and reporting. The analyst connects business processes, system configuration, data standards, integrations, security, support, training, and continuous improvement.

Present this as studied knowledge, not prior system ownership.

## Requirements Approach

1. Identify stakeholders, decisions, current workflow, pain points, controls, and reporting needs.
2. Separate business, functional, data, security, integration, reporting, and non-functional requirements.
3. Define acceptance criteria, assumptions, dependencies, priority, owner, and traceability.
4. Validate through interviews, workshops, process walkthroughs, prototypes, and review sessions.
5. Assess configuration before customization and document downstream impacts.
6. Maintain decisions, changes, unresolved questions, test evidence, and approvals.

## Data Governance Framework

- Establish field definitions, owners, naming conventions, coding structures, valid values, and mandatory fields.
- Define source-of-truth systems, lineage, update frequency, retention, access, and quality rules.
- Monitor completeness, consistency, validity, uniqueness, timeliness, and referential integrity.
- Assign remediation ownership and distinguish warnings from blocking errors.
- Maintain a data dictionary, business glossary, exception log, and change history.

## Access and Security Framework

- Use role-based access and least privilege.
- Separate user, approver, administrator, and reporting permissions.
- Require documented approvals, periodic access reviews, timely removal, and auditability.
- Test permissions with representative roles and protect sensitive financial, procurement, employee, and contract data.

## Project Controls Vocabulary

- **WBS:** Hierarchical decomposition of project scope into manageable work packages.
- **Baseline:** Approved scope, schedule, or cost reference used to measure change and performance.
- **Forecast:** Current estimate of future schedule or cost outcome based on known information.
- **Risk:** Possible future event; **issue:** current problem requiring action.
- **Earned value:** Framework comparing planned value, earned value, and actual cost.
- **Change control:** Documented assessment, approval, implementation, and traceability of changes.

Explain concepts accurately but state clearly that professional ownership is not verified.

## Training and Adoption Approach

- Keep documentation versioned and aligned with releases and governance decisions.
