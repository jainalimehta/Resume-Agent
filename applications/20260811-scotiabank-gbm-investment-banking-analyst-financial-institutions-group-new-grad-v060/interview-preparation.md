## Accounting Fundamentals

### Three-Statement Connections

- Net income from the income statement flows into retained earnings and begins the cash-flow statement's operating reconciliation.
- Non-cash expenses such as depreciation are added back in operating cash flow.
- Capital expenditure reduces cash and increases property, plant, and equipment before depreciation.
- Working-capital increases generally use cash; decreases generally release cash.
- Debt issuance increases cash and debt; interest affects earnings and cash flow over time.
- Share issuance increases cash and equity; repurchases reduce both.

### Common Walk-Through: Depreciation Rises by $10

Assume a 25% tax rate:

1. EBIT falls by $10.
2. Taxes fall by $2.50.
3. Net income falls by $7.50.
4. Cash-flow statement starts $7.50 lower, then adds back $10 of depreciation.
5. Cash rises by $2.50 relative to the original case.
6. PP&E falls by $10 and cash rises by $2.50, so assets fall by $7.50.
7. Retained earnings falls by $7.50; the balance sheet balances.

### Enterprise Value and Equity Value

Simplified bridge:

`Enterprise Value = Equity Value + Debt + Preferred Stock + Non-controlling Interest - Cash`

Enterprise value represents value attributable to all capital providers; equity value belongs to common shareholders.

## Financial-Institution Accounting

### Banks

- Loans are assets; deposits and wholesale funding are liabilities.
- Net interest income is interest income minus interest expense.
- Net interest margin is commonly net interest income divided by average interest-earning assets.
- Provision for credit losses reduces pre-tax income and increases the allowance for expected credit losses.
- Non-performing or impaired loans indicate credit deterioration.
- Tangible common equity and tangible book value remove goodwill and selected intangibles.

### IFRS 9 Expected Credit Losses — Conceptual

- Stage 1: performing assets; generally 12-month expected credit losses.
- Stage 2: significant increase in credit risk; lifetime expected credit losses.
- Stage 3: credit-impaired assets; lifetime expected credit losses with impaired-interest treatment.

Do not present this as accounting-practice experience.

## Valuation Fundamentals

### Standard Methods

- Trading comparable companies: applies peer multiples to the subject company's metrics.
- Precedent transactions: uses acquisition multiples and may reflect control premiums and deal conditions.
- DCF: discounts forecast unlevered free cash flow at WACC and adds terminal value.
- LBO: tests sponsor returns based on entry price, debt paydown, operations, and exit value.
- Sum-of-the-parts: values distinct businesses separately.

### Why Banks Are Different

Conventional EV/EBITDA and unlevered DCF are often less useful because deposits and debt are operating inputs, interest is core revenue/cost, and regulatory capital constrains growth and distributions.

Common bank methods:

- Price-to-book value.
- Price-to-tangible book value.
- Price-to-earnings.
- Dividend discount model.
- Residual income or excess-return model.

Key relationship: a bank sustainably earning return on equity above its cost of equity should generally trade above book value, all else equal.

### Insurer Valuation

- Price-to-book and price-to-earnings.
- Normalized ROE.
- Dividend discount or excess-return approaches.
- Embedded-value measures for life insurers where relevant.
- Sum-of-the-parts for diversified groups.

## FIG Business Models and KPIs

### Banks

- Net interest income and net interest margin.
- Fee income and revenue mix.
- Efficiency ratio.
- Loan and deposit growth.
- Funding cost and deposit mix.
- Gross impaired or non-performing loans.
- Provision for credit losses and allowance coverage.
- ROE and return on tangible common equity.
- Tangible book value per share.
- CET1 capital generation and distribution capacity.

### Property and Casualty Insurers

- Gross and net written premiums.
- Earned premiums.
- Loss ratio.
- Expense ratio.
- Combined ratio = loss ratio + expense ratio.
- A combined ratio below 100% generally indicates underwriting profitability.
- Reserve development, catastrophe losses, investment income, book-value growth, and ROE.

### Life Insurers

- Premiums and fee income.
- New business value and embedded value where disclosed.
- Policyholder liabilities.
- Mortality, morbidity, lapse, and longevity assumptions.
- Investment spread, capital adequacy, and cash generation.

### Asset and Wealth Managers

- Assets under management or administration.
- Net flows.
- Fee rate and revenue yield.
- Performance fees.
- Operating leverage and compensation ratio.

### Payments and Specialty Finance

- Payment volume, take rate, transactions, fraud/loss rates.
- Originations, receivables, credit losses, funding costs, leverage, and securitization access.

## Capital, Liquidity, and Regulation

### Bank Capital

`CET1 Ratio = CET1 Capital / Risk-Weighted Assets`

Understand CET1, Tier 1 capital, total capital, risk-weighted assets, leverage ratio, Liquidity Coverage Ratio, Net Stable Funding Ratio, stress testing, and distribution constraints.

The resulting supervisory CET1 expectation for Canadian D-SIBs is 11.0%, while sector CET1 averaged 13.5% as of April 30, 2026. This may create additional capital flexibility, but deployment still depends on risk, strategy, and institution-specific constraints. Verify these figures again immediately before the interview.

### FIG M&A Considerations

- EPS accretion or dilution.
- Tangible book value dilution and earn-back period.
- Pro forma CET1 and other capital ratios.
- Credit marks and allowance adequacy.
- Deposit quality and funding mix.
- Core deposit intangibles.
- Cost and revenue synergies.
- Customer, branch, product, and geographic overlap.
- Regulatory approval, competition, conduct, and integration risk.

### Bank-Merger Framework

1. Establish strategic rationale.
2. Determine purchase price and consideration.
3. Adjust target book value for fair-value and credit marks.
4. Estimate identifiable intangibles and goodwill.
5. Incorporate funding, synergies, and restructuring costs.
6. Calculate pro forma earnings and EPS impact.
7. Calculate tangible book value dilution and earn-back.
8. Assess pro forma capital and liquidity.
9. Sensitize credit, funding, and synergy assumptions.
10. Evaluate regulatory and execution risks.
