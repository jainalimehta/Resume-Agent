## Rotation foundations

### Product Management

Think through:

- Customer need and target segment.
- Value proposition and differentiation.
- Product economics: revenue, cost, margin, risk, and lifetime value.
- Acquisition, activation, usage, retention, and satisfaction.
- Channel, offer, pricing, servicing, compliance, and operational feasibility.
- Launch readiness, measurement, feedback, and iteration.

### Risk Management

Explain risk appetite as the amount and type of risk an organization is willing to accept in pursuit of objectives. A lending decision balances:

- Customer need and responsible access.
- Probability and severity of loss.
- Expected return and capital/resource use.
- Affordability, policy, legal and regulatory requirements.
- Fairness, model limitations, monitoring, overrides, and controls.

Do not claim professional credit-risk knowledge; present this as interview preparation.

### Data & Analytics

Use a disciplined cycle:

1. Clarify the decision and success measure.
2. Define data grain, sources, fields, time period, population, and exclusions.
3. Profile completeness, validity, consistency, uniqueness, timeliness, and lineage.
4. Reconcile metrics to trusted sources and document business rules.
5. Analyze drivers, segments, trends, outliers, and uncertainty.
6. Communicate the headline, evidence, limitations, recommendation, and follow-up measure.

### Collections

Collections aims to support customers while managing loss and operational effectiveness. Consider:

- Delinquency stage and customer circumstances.
- Contact strategy, channel, timing, and treatment.
- Promise-to-pay, cure, roll, recovery, and re-default outcomes.
- Affordability, vulnerability, fairness, privacy, and regulatory obligations.
- Treatment effectiveness, capacity, cost, and customer experience.

Do not claim collections modelling experience.

## Core KPI vocabulary

- Product: acquisition, activation, utilization, retention, attrition, balances, revenue, cost, margin, and customer satisfaction.
- Risk: approval, delinquency, default, loss, exposure, vintage, concentration, and risk-adjusted return.
- Collections: cure rate, roll rate, recovery, promise kept, re-default, contact rate, treatment effectiveness, and cost to collect.
- Data quality: completeness, validity, consistency, uniqueness, timeliness, reconciliation, issue aging, and recurrence.
- Reporting: refresh reliability, adoption, exceptions, decision time, and action completion.

## Business-case structure

1. Define the customer and business problem.
2. Establish a baseline and quantify the opportunity.
3. Compare options, including doing nothing.
4. Estimate benefits, costs, risks, dependencies, and timing.
5. State assumptions and sensitivity/scenario ranges.
6. Recommend an option with measures, ownership, implementation phases, and review points.

## Example case: improve digital product activation

- Clarify the product, target customers, current funnel, definition of activation, and desired outcome.
- Break the journey into application, approval, onboarding, first use, repeat use, and retention.
- Analyze drop-off by customer, product, channel, cohort, and time.
- Check friction, eligibility, communication, servicing, technical defects, and data quality.
- Recommend targeted interventions and a measured pilot with guardrails for risk and customer fairness.

## Example case: improve a reporting process

- Identify audience, decisions, current sources, manual steps, definitions, refresh timing, and pain points.
- Reconcile critical KPIs and establish owners and a data dictionary.
- Simplify output around decisions, exceptions, and actions.
- Test refreshes, permissions, calculations, and edge cases.
- Pilot with users, measure adoption and decision time, and document operating procedures.

## Likely behavioural questions

- Why BMO? Connect customer financial progress, innovation, analytics, risk discipline, inclusion, coaching, and Canadian business impact.
- Which rotation interests you most? Choose Data & Analytics initially, then explain why Product, Risk, and Collections context will make your recommendations stronger.
- How do you handle ambiguity? Clarify the decision, structure the problem, state assumptions, prioritize evidence, validate frequently, and communicate risk early.
