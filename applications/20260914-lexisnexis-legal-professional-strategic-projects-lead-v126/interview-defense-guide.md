# Interview Defense Guide — LexisNexis Strategic Projects Lead

## Candid Positioning

This is a stretch application. The referral may help secure a careful review, but it does not remove the experience gap. Present yourself as an early-career analyst with a credible strategy foundation—not as an experienced Strategic Projects Lead.

Your strongest promise is:

> I can clarify a business question, organize evidence, validate analysis, communicate a recommendation, and learn a new domain quickly. I have not yet led enterprise go-to-market strategy, and I would grow into that scope with clear priorities, feedback, and accountability.

## 60-Second Introduction

> I live in Toronto and completed a Master of Business Analytics at Edith Cowan University in Australia. At AYLA Solutions, I gathered and documented business and reporting requirements, supported SQL extraction and validation, prepared advanced Excel reports, and contributed performance insights while participating in Agile planning and reviews. Earlier support and administrative roles developed my client communication, workflow coordination, market-research support, and attention to accurate records. I have also built published analytics projects across e-commerce, healthcare, and workforce data, with validated SQL, AI-assisted interpretation, recommendations, and Power BI dashboard planning. I am early in my strategy career and have not yet used Salesforce or Tableau professionally or held a formal consulting role. What I can demonstrate is a disciplined way of learning: understand the decision, structure the data, test the result, and communicate what action the evidence supports.

## Story 1 — Moving from Operations into Analytics

Use this for “Tell me about yourself,” “Why strategy?” or “What shaped your career?”

**Situation:** Your earlier work at Trans Globe Education and Arihant Investment centred on client records, process updates, workflow support, communication, and accurate documentation.

**Motivation:** You became interested in how organizations could use the information already flowing through operations to improve decisions rather than only maintain records.

**Action:** You pursued a Master of Business Analytics in Australia and developed skills in systems analysis, business processes, databases, decision-making, project management, SQL, and Excel. You then applied that foundation as a Data Intern at AYLA Solutions and through published projects.

**Result:** You built a bridge from operational discipline to analytical work: gathering requirements, preparing reports, validating data, defining KPIs, and developing recommendations.

**Defensible answer:**

> My path into analytics started in operational roles where I saw how much decisions depend on accurate records, clear updates, and disciplined processes. That led me to complete a Master of Business Analytics in Australia. At AYLA Solutions, I began applying that learning to reporting requirements, SQL-supported extraction and validation, advanced Excel reports, and performance insights. My career is still developing, but the direction is consistent: I want to help teams move from operational information to clear, evidence-based action.

Do not add claims about leading teams, changing company strategy, improving revenue, or saving time unless you can substantiate them.

## Story 2 — Learning a New Problem Through GST Adoption Research

Use this for “Tell me about learning quickly,” “How do you approach market research?” or “How do you turn data into recommendations?”

**Situation:** The research examined small-business acceptance and use of online GST filing in Gujarat.

**Task:** Identify adoption patterns and common barriers, then document findings and recommendations.

**Actions:** Designed and conducted a Google Forms survey; cleaned and organized responses in Excel; used formulas, sorting, filtering, PivotTables, percentages, and charts; interpreted the patterns and documented recommendations.

**Result:** Completed the research with an A grade and produced a structured explanation of adoption barriers and possible responses.

**Defensible answer:**

> I began with a topic that required both subject learning and primary research: how small businesses were adopting online GST filing. I translated the broad topic into survey questions, collected responses through Google Forms, and used Excel formulas, filters, PivotTables, percentages, and charts to identify patterns and barriers. The important lesson was that analysis is useful only when the method is clear and the finding leads to a practical recommendation. That same structure—learn the context, define the question, organize the evidence, and explain the action—would guide me when studying a new LexisNexis market segment.

Do not invent the respondent count, locations beyond Gujarat, response rate, quantified findings, or business impact.

## Story 3 — Learning Across Domains Through Published Analytics Projects

Use this for “How do you learn independently?”, “How have you used AI?” or “Give an example of analytical ownership.”

**Situation:** You wanted to apply analytical methods beyond one domain.

**Task:** Build structured, inspectable analysis for e-commerce, healthcare, and workforce questions.

**Actions:** Created normalized PostgreSQL models; used constraints, joins, CTEs, rankings, segmentation, running totals, lag comparisons, and window functions; added repeatable validation and GitHub Actions checks; used Generative AI to help interpret verified SQL outputs and organize business recommendations; planned Power BI KPIs and dashboard layouts.

**Result:** Published three repositories with completed SQL, validation, AI-analysis, recommendation, and dashboard-planning material.

**Defensible answer:**

> I deliberately worked across e-commerce, healthcare, and workforce datasets so I would have to learn the meaning of each domain rather than repeat one familiar analysis. I first defined the entities and business rules, then built the PostgreSQL model and queries, checked the outputs, and only after validation used AI to help structure insights and recommendations. I treated AI as an interpretation aid, not a source of truth. The repositories show my work and also show the boundary accurately: the Power BI dashboard planning is complete, but interactive dashboards are not yet published.

Do not claim production deployment, real clients, confidential company data, large-scale datasets, implemented Power BI dashboards, or measured commercial outcomes.

## How to Answer the Major Gaps

### “Do you have consulting experience?”

> I have not held a formal consulting role. My closest transferable experience is gathering reporting requirements, communicating with stakeholders, researching a problem, structuring analysis, and presenting recommendations. I understand that consulting also requires hypothesis-led problem solving, executive communication, work-plan management, and influence without authority. Those are areas I am prepared to develop under feedback, but I would not label my prior employment as consulting.

### “Have you led strategic projects?”

> I have supported analytical work in an Agile environment and independently structured portfolio projects from question through model, analysis, validation, documentation, and recommendations. I have not led a cross-divisional enterprise strategy initiative. For a bounded project, I would clarify the decision and scope, define stakeholders and milestones, maintain actions, risks, and decisions, validate the evidence, and communicate progress and recommendations. I would seek guidance on governance and escalation expectations while building toward larger ownership.

### “What is your Salesforce experience?”

> I have not used Salesforce professionally. I understand the posting uses it to track activities, leads, opportunities, and sales. My transferable experience is maintaining accurate records, coordinating process updates, and working with structured reporting data. I would learn the team’s objects, lifecycle definitions, required fields, dashboards, data-quality rules, and reporting conventions before taking ownership.

### “What is your Tableau experience?”

> I have not used Tableau. My BI evidence is Power BI KPI and dashboard planning, supported by SQL analysis and validation; I have not published interactive Power BI dashboards either. The transferable foundation is defining the audience, KPI, grain, filters, visual purpose, and validation rule. I would learn Tableau hands-on, but I would not present planning knowledge as production-tool experience.

### “Do you use VLOOKUP?”

Current evidence confirms advanced Excel formulas, PivotTables, percentages, filters, and charts, but not VLOOKUP by name. Before an interview, complete and retain a small workbook showing exact-match and error-handled VLOOKUP, plus XLOOKUP or INDEX/MATCH if available. Until then, say:

> I have used advanced Excel formulas, PivotTables, filters, percentages, and charts. I do not have a documented work example specifically using VLOOKUP, so I would not overstate it. I understand the lookup purpose and can demonstrate it in a practical exercise.

### “Why should we hire you for a lead-titled role?”

> I would not ask you to treat my experience as equivalent to someone who has already led enterprise go-to-market programs. My case is based on learning trajectory and transferable execution: I gather and document requirements, work carefully with Excel and SQL, validate results, coordinate stakeholders and workflows, and translate analysis into recommendations. If the role requires immediate independent ownership of every strategic stream, I may not be the closest match. If the team can consider someone with a strong analytical foundation who can own bounded work, absorb feedback, and grow quickly, I can contribute with discipline and transparency.

## A Practical Strategy-Project Framework

For a case question, structure your answer as:

1. Clarify the decision, target customer segment, goal, time horizon, and constraints.
2. Establish the baseline using revenue, volume, conversion, retention, cost, productivity, or another agreed KPI.
3. Segment the opportunity and compare internal performance, customer needs, competitors, pricing, and channel evidence.
4. Develop options with assumptions, expected value, risks, dependencies, and resource needs.
5. Recommend a prioritized initiative and define owner, milestones, stakeholder cadence, and decision gates.
6. Track leading indicators in Salesforce or the approved system and lagging business outcomes in validated reporting.
7. Review results, capture learning, and adjust the initiative.

Present this as a framework you would use, not something you have already led at enterprise scale.

## Questions to Ask the Hiring Manager

- The posting title says Strategic Projects Lead while the body says Strategy Specialist. How is the role level defined in practice?
- Which market segment and business decision would be the first priority for this role?
- How much of the work is analysis versus stakeholder coordination and implementation?
- What level of independent strategic-project ownership is expected in the first 90 days?
- How are Sales and Marketing initiatives tracked in Salesforce, and which KPIs determine success?
- What does strong performance look like after six months?
- Where could an early-career candidate contribute immediately, and where would you expect the steepest learning curve?

## Freelance Evidence — Required Before Resume Inclusion

Your genuine freelance functions are confirmed, but none can be placed on this resume until the following facts are recorded for at least one real engagement:

1. Approximate month/year or year range.
2. Function: developer, architect, data engineer, or a truthful combination.
3. Client name or truthful confidential client type/industry.
4. Business or technical problem.
5. Tools and platforms actually used.
6. Work personally performed and level of ownership.
7. Deliverable provided and how it was used.
8. Measurable result, if substantiated.
9. People collaborated with and their real roles.

Only after those facts are supplied can we truthfully add collaboration with managers or staff engineers. Do not rehearse or use such a story before verifying it.

## Never Claim

- That AYLA Solutions employed you as a Business Analyst or Strategy Intern; your verified title is Data Intern.
- Formal consulting, enterprise strategy leadership, go-to-market ownership, Salesforce, Tableau, or product-pricing experience.
- Work with managers or staff engineers unless you provide the real engagement and collaborator details.
- Freelance dates, clients, tools, deliverables, outcomes, or metrics not recorded in the evidence ledger.
- Published interactive Power BI dashboards or `.pbix` files.
- Invented percentages, revenue effects, savings, stakeholder counts, team sizes, deadlines, or data volumes.
