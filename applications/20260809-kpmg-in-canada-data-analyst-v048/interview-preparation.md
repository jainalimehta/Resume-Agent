# KPMG in Canada — Data Analyst Interview Preparation

## Honest Positioning

This is an early-career stretch application. The strongest direct match is advanced Excel reporting, SQL analysis, data validation, requirements gathering, KPI reporting, stakeholder communication, and organized delivery. Budgeting, forecasting, monthly close, SmartView, VBA/macros, EPM/HFM, SAP HANA, and implemented Power BI dashboards are not verified experience. Discuss them as learning priorities, never as past work.

My analyst foundation comes from my Data Intern role at AYLA Solutions, where I gathered reporting requirements, supported SQL extraction and validation, prepared advanced Excel reports, and contributed dashboard insights for performance tracking. My portfolio adds financially relevant examples: monthly revenue and payment analysis in e-commerce, compensation KPIs in HR analytics, and customer transaction analysis for Bank of Baroda. Earlier operations roles strengthened my documentation, confidentiality, coordination, and client communication. I am interested in this KPMG role because it combines reporting discipline, analysis, business decision support, and continuous improvement. I can contribute reliable Excel and SQL work immediately while building proficiency in SmartView and KPMG’s enterprise planning environment.”

- KPMG's tax practice describes a modern, data-driven approach that combines tax knowledge, technology, process improvement, and analytics.
- The role connects monthly reporting with budgeting, forecasting, ad hoc analysis, and recommendations—the bridge between data preparation and business decisions that fits Jainali's development path.
- KPMG's values—Integrity, Excellence, Courage, Together, and For Better—fit careful data handling, continuous learning, constructive challenge, collaboration, and useful outcomes.
- Keep the answer specific: emphasize accurate reporting, transparent analysis, process efficiency, and service to internal teams.

## Role Knowledge to Learn

### Monthly Financial Reporting Cycle

1. Confirm the reporting calendar, source systems, definitions, owners, and cut-off dates.
2. Extract and reconcile actual results to approved sources.
3. Validate completeness, duplicates, account/category mappings, period dates, and unusual movements.
4. Compare actuals with budget, forecast, and prior periods.
5. Investigate material variances with business owners.
6. Prepare concise commentary: what changed, why, business impact, risk, and proposed action.
7. Obtain review, document adjustments, publish the report, and retain an audit trail.

### Variance Analysis Framework

For every material variance, separate the drivers where relevant:

- Volume: more or fewer transactions, clients, projects, or hours.
- Rate/price: a different billing, cost, or recovery rate.
- Mix: a shift between service lines, grades, regions, or categories.
- Timing: activity moved between reporting periods.
- Headcount/capacity: vacancies, hiring, utilization, or overtime.
- One-time items: corrections, accruals, exceptional costs, or reclassifications.

Conclude with an action: monitor, correct the data, update the forecast, escalate a risk, or investigate further.

### Budgeting and Forecasting

- Budget: an approved financial plan based on assumptions, priorities, capacity, and expected activity.
- Forecast: the latest expected outcome using actual results plus revised assumptions for remaining periods.
- Questions to ask: Which assumptions changed? Are drivers recurring? What is the full-year impact? What scenario range is reasonable? Who owns the corrective action?
- Honest response: “I have analyzed monthly revenue, payments, compensation, and transaction patterns, but I have not owned a corporate budget or forecast. I understand the core workflow and would learn KPMG's calendar, drivers, controls, and review standards quickly.”

### Tool Readiness

- Excel evidence to defend: advanced Excel reporting and pivot tables. Be prepared to explain data cleaning, sorting/filtering, formulas, pivot-table design, reconciliations, and chart selection using genuine examples.
- Study before interview: SUMIFS, COUNTIFS, XLOOKUP, INDEX/MATCH, IFERROR, date functions, conditional formatting, data validation, pivot charts, and basic Power Query. Do not claim proficiency until practised.
- Power BI: describe completed KPI, visualization, and dashboard planning only. Explain how requirements, measures, filters, drill-downs, refresh expectations, and validation would be designed.
- SmartView: an Oracle add-in used to retrieve and submit planning/financial data through Office. Learn its purpose and workflow; do not claim use.
- EPM/HFM: enterprise planning, consolidation, and financial reporting environments. Learn dimensional structures, scenarios, versions, entities, accounts, periods, and controls.
- SAP HANA/BusinessObjects: learn that HANA is a data platform and BusinessObjects supports enterprise reporting. Do not imply hands-on experience.
- VBA/macros: listed as an asset only. Say you have not used them professionally and would first look for a controlled, maintainable solution appropriate to the task.

## Likely Questions and Answer Direction

1. **How would you prepare a monthly financial report?** Use the seven-step cycle above; emphasize source reconciliation, variance drivers, review, and audit trail.
2. **How do you ensure accuracy?** Define rules, profile inputs, reconcile totals, test exceptions, investigate anomalies, document assumptions, and obtain review.
3. **Tell me about an ad hoc analysis.** Use Bank of Baroda or e-commerce; start with the decision question, then method, validation, insight, and recommendation.
4. **How do you manage competing deadlines?** Clarify priority and due date, break work into checkpoints, track dependencies, communicate risk early, and protect review time.
5. **How would you improve a recurring report?** Confirm user decisions, remove unused content, standardize definitions, reduce manual handling, add controlled checks, document ownership, and measure reliability/timeliness.
6. **What is your Power BI experience?** State dashboard planning and KPI design accurately; explain the planned audience, measures, visuals, filters, and validation. Do not claim an implemented dashboard.
7. **You have not used SmartView—how will you close the gap?** Connect concepts to Excel/SQL, study the environment and dimensional model, shadow an existing process, practise in a safe environment, document steps, and seek review before independent delivery.
8. **Why should we hire you without budgeting experience?** Emphasize verified analytical foundations, accuracy, learning agility, financial KPI work, and a clear understanding of the reporting/forecasting workflow; acknowledge the gap directly.

## Practical Case Approach

If given a workbook with an unexpected monthly expense increase:

1. Restate the question, materiality threshold, deadline, and comparison baseline.
2. Check row counts, totals, dates, duplicates, mappings, signs, and missing values.
3. Reconcile the report total to the source.
4. Segment by region, team, category, account, and period.
5. Separate volume, rate, mix, timing, and one-time drivers.
6. Validate the leading explanation with the owner or a second source.
7. Present a one-line conclusion, supporting table/chart, risk, and recommended next step.

## Questions to Ask the Interviewer

- Which monthly reports and decisions would this analyst support first?
- What are the most important financial drivers and recurring variance categories for the regional tax team?
- How are Excel, Power BI, SmartView, EPM/HFM, and SAP reporting used together in the current workflow?
- Where does the team see the greatest opportunity to streamline reporting or improve data quality?
- What would strong performance look like after the first 90 days?

## 30-60-90 Day Outline

- First 30 days: learn the reporting calendar, definitions, stakeholders, source systems, controls, and review expectations; reproduce an existing report with guidance.
- Days 31–60: support recurring reporting and ad hoc requests, document variance drivers, strengthen checks, and build confidence with SmartView and the planning environment.
- Days 61–90: independently own defined reporting tasks, recommend one practical process improvement, and create clear documentation or cross-training material.

## Final Guardrails

- Say `supported`, `contributed`, or `participated` for AYLA work; do not upgrade the internship title or ownership.
- Do not claim corporate budgeting, forecasting, monthly close, tax-practice experience, SmartView, VBA, EPM/HFM, SAP HANA, or implemented Power BI dashboards.
- Do not invent metrics, savings, dataset sizes, client names, team sizes, or timelines.
- Keep answers concise: context, task, actions, result, learning, and relevance to KPMG.
