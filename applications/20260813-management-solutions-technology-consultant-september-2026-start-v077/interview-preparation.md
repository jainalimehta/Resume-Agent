## Database topics to defend

- Relational model: entities, attributes, relationships, cardinality, primary keys, foreign keys, and constraints.
- Normalization: reduce duplication and update anomalies while preserving clear relationships; explain when reporting performance may require carefully governed denormalization.
- Integrity: entity integrity, referential integrity, uniqueness, allowed-value checks, null handling, and reconciliation.
- SQL: joins, aggregation, CTEs, window functions, ranking, running totals, lag comparisons, filtering, and ordering.
- Data quality: completeness, validity, consistency, uniqueness, accuracy, timeliness, and lineage.
- Performance foundation: filter early where appropriate, avoid unnecessary columns, understand indexes conceptually, inspect execution plans, and validate that optimization does not change results.
- Security foundation: least privilege, controlled access, approved environments, secrets protection, logging, and prompt incident escalation. Present these as principles, not professional cybersecurity experience.

## E-commerce database walkthrough

Be ready to explain:

1. Why customers, products, orders, line items, and payments are separate entities.
2. How primary and foreign keys preserve relationships.
3. Why an order can contain many line items and may have associated payment records.
4. How uniqueness and value checks prevent invalid records.
5. Why recognized revenue excludes pending, refunded, and cancelled activity.
6. How validation queries and GitHub Actions checks make analytical rules repeatable.
7. What you would add for production readiness: access controls, migrations, monitoring, backups, tests, documentation, and performance review—without claiming you implemented them.

## Technology-consulting case structure

1. Clarify the business objective, stakeholders, current pain points, scope, constraints, risks, and success measures.
2. Map the current process, applications, data flows, roles, controls, handoffs, and exceptions.
3. Diagnose root causes across people, process, data, technology, and governance.
4. Define future-state requirements and prioritize them by value, risk, dependency, and feasibility.
5. Compare solution options: process improvement, configuration, integration, data remediation, platform change, or custom development.
6. Recommend a target approach with architecture principles, delivery phases, owners, milestones, risks, controls, and measures.
7. Plan testing, change management, training, deployment, stabilization, and ongoing governance.

## Likely questions

- Why technology consulting? Emphasize varied business problems, databases and systems, structured learning, teamwork, and practical transformation.
- Why Management Solutions? Connect high-profile projects, multidisciplinary teams, formal training investment, merit-based growth, and partnership opportunities.
- How do you learn quickly? Use a real project example only: define the gap, select authoritative resources, practice, validate, seek feedback, and document learning.
- How do you handle an unfamiliar technology? Clarify the use case, architecture, data, controls, interfaces, and success criteria; build a safe prototype or exercise; test and request review.
- How do you maintain quality? Explicit rules, traceable requirements, validation, peer review, reproducible checks, exception handling, and documented limitations.
- Tell me about teamwork. Use AYLA Agile participation or Trans Globe coordination without inventing team size or leadership.
