# Vretta — Business Analyst Interview Preparation

## 60-Second Introduction

“I am an early-career Business Analytics graduate living in Toronto. At AYLA Solutions in Australia, I gathered and documented business and reporting requirements, supported SQL extraction and validation, prepared advanced Excel reports, contributed dashboard insights, and participated in Agile planning and reviews. My published portfolio extends that foundation through relational modelling, KPI definitions, repeatable validation, AI-assisted interpretation, and Power BI dashboard planning. I also worked at Trans Globe Education, coordinating documentation and process updates with students and institutions. Vretta interests me because it combines client understanding, product decisions, data, and meaningful education outcomes.”

## Four Stories to Prepare

1. **Requirements into analytical work:** AYLA — explain the operational/reporting need, questions you asked, what you documented, and how the output remained aligned. Do not invent stakeholders or outcomes.
2. **Metric definition and validation:** E-Commerce Sales Analytics — explain recognized revenue, integrity rules, edge cases, monthly trends, and why a clear definition matters to product reporting.
3. **Education-sector coordination:** Trans Globe — explain accurate records and communication across students and institutions, including how you handled changing process information.
4. **User adoption and barriers:** Online GST Filing Adoption Research — explain survey design, Excel cleaning/analysis, adoption patterns, barriers, and recommendations.

## A Strong Stakeholder-Session Framework

1. Clarify the business outcome, affected users, current process, and decision to be supported.
2. Identify stakeholders, assumptions, constraints, terminology, and known pain points.
3. Separate needs from proposed solutions.
4. Capture functional requirements, data requirements, business rules, exceptions, dependencies, and non-functional considerations.
5. Define measurable acceptance criteria and relevant KPIs.
6. Play back the requirements, resolve conflicts, document decisions, and maintain traceability through refinement and delivery.

## Product and Education Metrics to Understand

- User activation and onboarding completion
- Assessment completion and abandonment
- Time on task and response latency
- Error, support-ticket, and incident rates
- Feature adoption and repeat usage
- Accessibility-related issues
- Teacher/institution satisfaction
- Data completeness and reporting timeliness

Do not claim to have measured these at Vretta or professionally. Explain that metric definitions must reflect the intended learning or operational decision, protect privacy, and avoid treating engagement alone as proof of learning.

## Honest Gap Answers

### “Have you worked in product management?”

“I have not owned a product roadmap, backlog, or prioritization process. My direct foundation is gathering and documenting business/reporting requirements, participating in Agile planning and reviews, and translating operational needs into analytical work. I understand that a product-facing analyst must add user context, acceptance criteria, dependencies, measurable outcomes, and traceability while supporting the product manager's decisions.”

### “Have you built Power BI dashboards?”

“My verified work covers KPI definitions, visualization requirements, and dashboard planning. I have not published interactive dashboards or `.pbix` files. I can defend the business questions, data requirements, metric logic, visual choices, and validation approach behind each plan.”

### “Do you use GitLab, Notion, or Trello?”

“I have not used those tools in verified work. I have used Git, GitHub, GitHub Actions, Microsoft Office, Google Workspace, and Agile documentation practices. I would learn Vretta's workflow by understanding its issue structure, naming conventions, permissions, templates, and definition of done rather than assuming all tools operate identically.”

### “Why education technology?”

“My role at Trans Globe Education showed me how strongly clear information, reliable records, and well-coordinated processes affect a student's experience. I am interested in Vretta because assessment and learning platforms operate at a larger scale: the quality of requirements, data, accessibility, and reporting can influence how institutions support learners. I would bring that motivation without pretending I already have assessment-product expertise.”

## Technical and Analytical Questions to Practise

- Explain how you would convert a vague request into measurable requirements and acceptance criteria.
- Explain `INNER JOIN` versus `LEFT JOIN` using students, assessments, and submissions.
- Find learners with no submitted assessment while preserving all enrolled learners.
- Define and validate completion rate, abandonment rate, and turnaround time.
- Use a window function to rank outcomes within an institution or compare performance over time.
- Explain how survey bias, missing data, and ambiguous categories can weaken a recommendation.
- Explain the difference between correlation, product usage, and actual learning improvement.

## Strong Questions for Vretta

1. Which client and product decisions would this Business Analyst support most often?
2. How are stakeholder requests documented, refined, prioritized, and traced through sprint delivery?
3. Which product and learning-related outcomes matter most, and how are their definitions governed?
4. How do Business Analysts collaborate with product managers, designers, technologists, and psychometricians?
5. What would strong performance look like during the first 90 days?

## Interview Guardrails

- Say `participated in Agile planning and reviews`, not `owned sprint planning`.
- Say `Power BI KPI and dashboard planning`, not implemented dashboards.
- Say `education-sector coordination`, not education-technology product experience.
- Do not claim GitLab, Notion, Trello, Tableau, roadmap ownership, backlog prioritization, or executive advisory experience.
- Describe AYLA as `Data Intern`, never Business Analyst Intern.
