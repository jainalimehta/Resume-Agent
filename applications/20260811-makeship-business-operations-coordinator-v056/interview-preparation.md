## Understand the 21-Day Campaign Lifecycle

This is an inferred preparation model based on the posting, not inside knowledge of Makeship’s exact process.

1. Campaign concept and partner requirements are confirmed.
2. Product design, price, quantities, assets, dates, and approvals are coordinated.
3. Product and campaign records are configured in the required systems.
4. Launch-readiness checks confirm page content, links, pricing, inventory assumptions, payment flow, and tracking.
5. Campaign launches for the 21-day funding window.
6. Performance, exceptions, support issues, and changes are monitored.
7. Funded campaigns move into production coordination.
8. Manufacturing, quality, logistics, and customer-communication milestones are tracked.
9. Campaign results and operational lessons are documented.

## Cross-Functional Coordination Framework

For every workstream, track:

- Deliverable.
- Owner.
- Due date.
- Status.
- Dependency.
- Risk or blocker.
- Decision required.
- Evidence/link.
- Next action.

When priorities conflict:

1. Confirm customer/campaign impact and deadline.
2. Identify dependencies and the cost of delay.
3. Clarify the decision owner.
4. Present options and tradeoffs.
5. Document the agreed priority.
6. Communicate changes to affected teams.

## Handling an Urgent Internal Request

1. Acknowledge the request quickly.
2. Clarify the problem, impact, urgency, affected users/campaigns, and desired outcome.
3. Check known documentation and recent changes.
4. Reproduce or validate the issue where safe.
5. Resolve within scope or route to the correct owner with evidence.
6. Provide a status and realistic next update time.
7. Confirm resolution with the requester.
8. Document the fix and preventive action.

Good sentence:

“Fast response does not mean skipping controls; I would separate immediate containment from permanent resolution.”

## SOP Writing Framework

A useful SOP should include:

- Purpose and scope.
- Trigger and frequency.
- Roles and responsibilities.
- Required inputs and access.
- Numbered steps with screenshots or examples where appropriate.
- Decision points and exception handling.
- Quality checks.
- Escalation path.
- Expected output.
- Owner, version, approval, and last-review date.

Test the SOP by asking a colleague unfamiliar with the process to follow it and record unclear steps.

## Workflow Mapping

Use a simple structure:

- **Trigger:** What starts the workflow?
- **Inputs:** What information or approvals are required?
- **Steps:** Who does what, in what order?
- **Decisions:** What conditions change the path?
- **Systems:** Where is information entered or retrieved?
- **Controls:** What prevents or detects errors?
- **Outputs:** What proves completion?
- **Exceptions:** Who resolves failures?

Distinguish the current-state workflow from the proposed future state.

## Low-Code/No-Code Automation — Honest Answer

“I have not yet built production automations in Airtable or Zapier. My closest technical evidence is relational database modelling, SQL-based data preparation and validation, GitHub Actions checks, and process documentation. Before automating a workflow, I would map the trigger, inputs, decision rules, system of record, outputs, exception path, permissions, and monitoring. I would first build and test a small low-risk workflow, validate edge cases, document ownership, and keep a manual recovery path.”

Study these concepts:

- Trigger, action, filter, condition, and branching.
- Record identifiers and idempotency.
- Field mapping and schema changes.
- Authentication and least-privilege access.
- Rate limits and retries.
- Error logs, alerts, and manual recovery.
- Test versus production environments.
- Audit trail and change control.

## Example Practice Automation

Treat this as a proposed design, not claimed experience:

**Goal:** Notify the campaign owner when required launch fields are incomplete.

1. Trigger when a campaign record moves to `Launch Review`.
2. Check required fields such as approved assets, price, launch date, and manufacturing status.
3. If complete, update readiness status and record the check time.
4. If incomplete, create an exception list and notify the assigned owner.
5. Log the automation result and allow retry after corrections.
6. Monitor false positives and update rules only through controlled changes.

## Data-Quality Framework

Check:

- Completeness of required campaign and product fields.
- Uniqueness of campaign, product, and order identifiers.
- Validity of dates, prices, currencies, statuses, and categories.
- Consistency between systems.
- Referential integrity between campaign, product, order, and payment records.
- Timeliness of status updates.
- Reconciliation of counts and financial totals.

If a check fails, record affected items, severity, owner, resolution, and retest evidence.
