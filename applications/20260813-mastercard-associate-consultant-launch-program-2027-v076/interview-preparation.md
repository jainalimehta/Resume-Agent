## Business Experimentation framework

When given an experiment case:

1. Clarify the business decision, intervention, target population, timeframe, constraints, and economic objective.
2. State a falsifiable hypothesis and define primary, secondary, and guardrail metrics.
3. Define the unit of treatment: customer, account, store, geography, product, or time period.
4. Select test and control groups and assess pre-period comparability.
5. Check contamination, spillovers, cannibalization, seasonality, concurrent initiatives, and selection bias.
6. Estimate incremental impact, uncertainty, heterogeneity, and practical significance—not only correlation.
7. Translate the result into financial value, operational feasibility, risk, and rollout conditions.
8. Recommend implement, refine/retest, target selected segments, or stop; state limitations and monitoring needs.

## Example: new-store location

- Objective: maximize incremental profit, not gross sales alone.
- Measures: incremental store revenue, lost nearby-store/online sales, margin, fixed and variable cost, payback, and customer reach.
- Design: compare candidate or opened stores with similar control markets using pre- and post-period trends.
- Risks: site-selection bias, local events, competitor changes, seasonality, marketing differences, and online cannibalization.
- Recommendation: prioritize sites with positive incremental contribution after cannibalization and cost, then pilot and monitor.

## Example: credit-card acquisition bonus

- Objective: increase valuable, sustained card usage profitably.
- Measures: activation, purchase frequency/value, retention, revolving behaviour where appropriate, reward cost, credit risk, and incremental profit.
- Design: randomized or carefully matched bonus levels with defined eligibility and observation windows.
- Risks: customers who would have applied anyway, short-term gaming, adverse selection, fairness, regulatory constraints, and reward-cost leakage.
- Recommendation: target the lowest bonus that produces durable incremental value in eligible segments.

## Strategy & Transformation case structure

1. Clarify the client's objective, market, customer, economics, capabilities, and definition of success.
2. Diagnose external factors and internal performance using evidence.
3. Structure drivers such as customer proposition, product, channel, operations, data, technology, people, governance, and financial impact.
4. Generate options and compare attractiveness, feasibility, investment, risk, and time to value.
5. Recommend a prioritized path with milestones, owners, measures, risks, and immediate next steps.
