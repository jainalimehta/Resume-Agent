## Project Lifecycle

### Initiation

- Confirm project number, client, scope, deliverables, contractual dates, stakeholders, communication paths, and file structure.
- Record assumptions, open questions, initial risks, and required disciplines.

### Planning

- Break deliverables into milestones and tasks.
- Record owners, planned dates, dependencies, internal review time, client inputs, and approvals.
- Establish the action log, deliverable register, RAID log, change log, meeting cadence, and document register.

### Execution

- Schedule meetings, circulate agendas, capture decisions, distribute minutes, and track actions.
- Coordinate inputs and technical reviews without changing technical content independently.
- Maintain current schedules, deliverable status, correspondence, and file revisions.

### Monitoring and control

- Compare current status with the approved plan, scope, dates, and budget information supplied by the Project Manager.
- Identify late inputs, conflicting instructions, version problems, unresolved questions, and possible scope changes.
- Escalate early with evidence, impact, owner, decision required, and proposed next step.

### Close-out

- Confirm final deliverables, approvals, outstanding actions, record documentation, manuals or warranties when applicable, and organized archives.
- Follow INVIRO's naming, quality, retention, and approval procedures.

## Essential Trackers

### Action log

`ID | Source | Action | Owner | Assigned | Due | Status | Dependency | Update | Closed`

### Deliverable register

`ID | Discipline | Deliverable | Revision | Owner | Internal Review | Client Due | Status | Submitted | Accepted`

### RAID log

- Risk: possible future event; record probability, impact, owner, response, and review date.
- Assumption: statement treated as true for planning but requiring validation.
- Issue: current problem requiring action.
- Dependency: input or work required before another task can proceed.

### Document register

`Document No. | Discipline | Title | Revision | Status | Prepared | Checked | Approved | Date | Recipient | Transmittal`

The control objective is that everyone can identify the current document, what changed, who approved it, and where its audit trail lives.

## Meeting Minutes

Record the meeting purpose and date, attendees, concise discussion summary, decisions, actions with one owner and due date, risks/issues, and next meeting. Separate discussion from confirmed decisions. Distribute promptly and follow the company's correction and approval process.

## Schedule Concepts

- Milestone: significant zero-duration checkpoint.
- Task: work with duration, owner, dates, and dependencies.
- Dependency: logical relationship between tasks; finish-to-start is a common example.
- Critical path: sequence governing the earliest completion date.
- Baseline: approved schedule used for comparison.
- Float: time an activity can move without affecting a defined successor or finish date.
- Look-ahead: near-term view of work, inputs, constraints, and decisions.

Professional follow-up example:

> The electrical input is due Thursday and is required for Monday's coordinated review. Please confirm completion by Thursday noon or identify the blocker today so the Project Manager can assess the milestone impact.

## Budget Concepts

You do not have verified project-budget experience. Explain concepts without claiming ownership:

- Budget: approved expected project cost or fee allocation.
- Actual: cost recorded to date.
- Commitment: amount contractually committed but not necessarily paid.
- Forecast: current estimate of final cost.
- Variance: difference between plan and actual or forecast.

A coordinator may enter approved figures, reconcile trackers, flag missing information, and route exceptions. The Project Manager or authorized cost owner makes commercial decisions.

## Change Notice and Change Order Workflow

Terminology and authority vary by contract, so use INVIRO's definitions.

1. Receive or identify a potential change.
2. Log its source, date, description, affected discipline, reason, and status.
3. Gather technical input from authorized reviewers.
4. Record possible scope, schedule, cost, procurement, and document impacts.
5. Route for approval under the project's authority matrix.
6. Update controlled records only after authorization.
7. Communicate approved direction and retain the audit trail.

Never treat a verbal request as approved direction or implement an undocumented change.

## Construction Document Terms

- Drawing: graphical design information with number, discipline, revision, and issue status.
- Specification: written requirements for systems, materials, quality, installation, and testing.
- RFI: formal question used to clarify incomplete, unclear, or conflicting project information.
- Submittal: contractor or supplier information submitted for review against project requirements.
- Shop drawing: fabrication or installation information prepared by a contractor, manufacturer, fabricator, or supplier.
- Transmittal: formal record of documents or information sent.
- Revision: controlled update; superseded versions must not be distributed as current.

Procore's official documentation says its Submittals tool organizes submittal logs and can include shop drawings and product-catalogue pages while tracking status and responsible action. Study: https://support.procore.com/products/online/user-guide/project-level/submittals

## Submittal Coordination

1. Confirm the required submittal against the specification or register.
2. Log number, discipline, specification section, revision, submitter, reviewer, dates, and status.
3. Check administrative completeness—not technical acceptability.
4. Route to the authorized technical reviewer.
5. Track turnaround and follow up before the due date.
6. Record the official disposition using INVIRO's terminology.
7. Distribute to authorized recipients and retain the audit trail.
8. Preserve revision history on resubmission.
