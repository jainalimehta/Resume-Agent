# Aecon — Analyst, Business Interview Preparation

## 60-Second Introduction

“I am an early-career Business Analytics graduate living in Toronto. At AYLA Solutions in Australia, I gathered and documented business and reporting requirements, supported SQL extraction and validation, prepared advanced Excel reports, contributed dashboard insights, and participated in Agile planning and reviews. My published portfolio extends that foundation through relational data modelling, integrity rules, repeatable checks, AI-assisted interpretation of verified SQL findings, and Power BI dashboard planning. I am interested in AeconONE because it brings process improvement, data reliability, and digital enablement together in support of consistent project delivery.”

## Four Evidence Stories to Prepare

1. **Requirements and process documentation:** Use AYLA. Explain the operational or reporting need, how you clarified it, what you documented, and how the analytical work stayed aligned. Do not invent stakeholders or outcomes.
2. **Data quality and troubleshooting mindset:** Use the e-commerce project. Explain primary/foreign keys, checks, recognized-revenue logic, edge cases, repeatable validation, and why trustworthy rules come before reporting.
3. **Operational insight from connected data:** Use the healthcare project. Explain how linked appointments, treatments, billing, and claims supported analysis of completion, cost, workload, and claims patterns.
4. **Workflow coordination and adoption:** Use Trans Globe. Explain how accurate records and timely process updates helped students and institutions stay coordinated.

## Likely Gap Questions

### “How much Power Platform experience do you have?”

“I do not yet have verified hands-on Power Automate or Power Apps experience, so I would not present myself as production-ready in those tools. My transferable foundation includes process mapping, requirements, relational data logic, validation, and documentation. I am preparing to learn Power Automate systematically by translating a mapped process into triggers, conditions, actions, exception handling, testing, and support documentation.”

### “Have you used Microsoft Fabric?”

“I have not used Microsoft Fabric hands-on. I do understand the underlying analytical concerns from PostgreSQL work: source structure, ingestion logic, transformation, relational modelling, data quality, measures, and reliable reporting outputs. I would build Fabric depth around OneLake, pipelines, semantic models, refresh monitoring, and access controls without claiming experience I have not yet earned.”

### “Have you maintained production automations?”

“I have not maintained live production automation workflows. My closest evidence is maintaining repeatable validation and GitHub Actions checks across published projects. That work taught me to define expected behaviour, catch failures consistently, document logic, and protect output quality. In production support, I would add monitoring, incident triage, change control, rollback awareness, and clear escalation.”

### “What is your Power BI level?”

“My verified experience is Power BI KPI, visualization, and dashboard planning. I have not published interactive dashboards or `.pbix` files. I can explain the business questions, KPI definitions, data requirements, visual choices, and validation plan in my repositories, and I am continuing toward hands-on implementation.”

## Knowledge to Build Before Interview

- **Process mapping:** SIPOC, swimlanes, current-state versus future-state maps, handoffs, controls, exceptions, bottlenecks, and acceptance criteria.
- **Lean basics:** value versus waste, the eight wastes, root-cause analysis, 5 Whys, standard work, and value-stream thinking.
- **Power Automate:** triggers, actions, conditions, expressions, variables, approvals, connectors, retries, error handling, environments, solutions, and run history.
- **Power Apps:** canvas versus model-driven apps, forms, data sources, validation, roles, delegation, testing, and deployment concepts.
- **Microsoft Fabric:** OneLake, Data Factory pipelines, lakehouses, warehouses, notebooks, semantic models, lineage, refresh, and governance.
- **Production support:** monitoring, incident severity, reproducibility, logs, root cause, workarounds, fixes, retesting, change control, deployment, rollback, and handover.
- **Utility infrastructure context:** learn Aecon Utilities' major markets and how project teams might use data intake, approvals, document processing, planning, quality, and reporting workflows.

## A Practical Process-Mapping Answer

“I would begin by defining the process boundary, customer or user, desired outcome, inputs, outputs, and owners. I would observe the current workflow and document steps, handoffs, systems, decisions, exceptions, controls, wait times, and pain points. After validating the map with users, I would distinguish value-adding work from repetition or risk, prioritize an improvement opportunity, and define a future state. Before automation, I would specify business rules, data fields, permissions, exception paths, audit needs, acceptance criteria, and success measures.”

## Strong Questions for AeconONE

1. Which operational workflows would this analyst support first, and what currently causes the most rework or delay?
2. How does AeconONE decide whether a process should be standardized, simplified, automated, or left unchanged?
3. How are Power Platform solutions governed across environments, releases, security, ownership, and support?
4. What does successful post-go-live stabilization look like, and which measures indicate solution health?
5. How do project teams, Continuous Improvement, Quality, Planning, and technical teams approve process and data requirements?

## Interview Guardrails

- Say `participated in Agile sprint planning and reviews`, not `led Agile delivery`.
- Say `Power BI KPI and dashboard planning`, not `developed or maintained Power BI dashboards`.
- Say `AI-assisted analysis grounded in verified SQL outputs`, not `built AI automation`.
- Say `relational modelling and repeatable validation`, not `built Fabric data pipelines`.
- Say plainly that Power Automate, Power Apps, Fabric, Python, SharePoint, production automation, and Lean implementation are learning areas.
