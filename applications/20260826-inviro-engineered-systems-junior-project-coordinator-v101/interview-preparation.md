## Understand INVIRO's Environment

- **ICI:** industrial, commercial, and institutional buildings. Examples can include manufacturing or warehouse facilities, offices and retail, schools, hospitals, and other public/institutional buildings.
- **Mechanical engineering scope:** may include HVAC, plumbing, drainage, fire protection, controls, and equipment coordination. Exact scope varies by project.
- **Electrical engineering scope:** may include power distribution, lighting, fire alarm, communications, controls, emergency power, and related systems.
- **Multidisciplinary coordination:** drawings, specifications, equipment, schedules, and decisions must stay aligned across architectural, mechanical, electrical, structural, client, consultant, contractor, and supplier inputs.
- **Your boundary:** a coordinator controls information, status, dates, follow-up, and documentation. Engineers and authorized reviewers make technical/design decisions.

## Project Lifecycle and Your Coordinator Controls

### 1. Initiation

- Confirm project number, client, scope, deliverables, stakeholders, contractual dates, communication path, and file structure.
- Record assumptions, open questions, initial risks, and required technical disciplines.

### 2. Planning

- Break deliverables into work packages and milestones.
- Record owners, planned dates, dependencies, review time, client inputs, and approval requirements.
- Establish the document register, meeting cadence, action log, risk/issue log, and change log.

### 3. Execution

- Schedule meetings, circulate agendas, capture decisions, distribute minutes, and track actions.
- Coordinate document inputs and reviews without changing technical content independently.
- Maintain schedules, deliverable status, correspondence, and current file versions.

### 4. Monitoring and Control

- Compare actual status with planned dates, scope, and budget information supplied by the Project Manager.
- Identify late inputs, conflicting instructions, version problems, unresolved technical questions, and potential scope changes.
- Escalate early with the impact, owner, decision needed, and proposed next step.

### 5. Close-out

- Confirm final deliverables, approvals, outstanding actions, record drawings/documents, warranties or manuals where applicable, and organized archives.
- Ensure the close-out package follows INVIRO's naming, retention, and quality procedures.

## Essential Logs and Documents

### Action Log

`Action ID | Meeting/Source | Action | Owner | Assigned Date | Due Date | Status | Dependency | Update | Closed Date`

### Deliverable Register

`Deliverable ID | Discipline | Title | Revision | Owner | Internal Review | Client Due | Status | Dependency | Submission Date | Acceptance`

### RAID Log

- **Risk:** a possible future event; record probability, impact, owner, response, and review date.
- **Assumption:** something treated as true for planning but needing validation.
- **Issue:** a current problem requiring action.
- **Dependency:** work or input required before another task can proceed.

PMI describes a risk register as a document or database listing project risks and useful management information. Review PMI's [risk-register overview](https://www.pmi.org/learning/library/managing-risk-project-portfolio-7297).

### Meeting Minutes

Use: project/date, attendees, purpose, concise discussion summary, decisions, actions with one owner and one due date, risks/issues, next meeting. Send minutes promptly, request corrections within the company's defined period, and never rewrite a disputed decision silently.

### Document Register

`Document Number | Discipline | Title | Revision | Status | Prepared By | Checked By | Approved By | Date | Sent To | Transmittal | Supersedes`

The rule is simple: the team must know which document is current, what changed, who approved it, and where the audit trail lives.

## Schedule Knowledge

- **Milestone:** a significant zero-duration checkpoint, such as design submission or client approval.
- **Task:** work with duration, owner, start/end dates, and dependencies.
- **Dependency:** a logical relationship between tasks; a common example is finish-to-start.
- **Critical path:** the sequence of activities that determines the earliest project finish; delay on it can delay completion unless mitigated.
- **Baseline:** the approved schedule used for comparison.
- **Float:** time a task can move without affecting a defined successor or project finish, depending on the float type.
- **Look-ahead:** a short-term view of upcoming work, decisions, and constraints.

Strong follow-up language:

> The electrical input is due Thursday and is required before the coordinated package review on Monday. Please confirm completion by Thursday at noon or identify the blocker today so the Project Manager can assess the milestone impact.

## Budget Knowledge

You do not have verified project-budget experience, so explain the concepts without claiming ownership.

- **Budget:** approved expected project cost or fee allocation.
- **Actual cost:** cost recorded to date.
- **Commitment:** amount contractually committed but not necessarily paid.
- **Forecast:** current estimate of final cost based on known performance and changes.
- **Variance:** difference between budget/plan and actual or forecast.

A coordinator may update approved figures, reconcile logs, flag missing data, and route exceptions. The Project Manager or authorized cost owner decides corrective action.

## Change Notice and Change Order Workflow

Terminology varies by company and contract, so use INVIRO's definitions.

1. Receive or identify a potential change.
2. Log the source, date, description, affected discipline, reason, and current status.
3. Gather technical input from authorized reviewers.
4. Record possible scope, schedule, cost, procurement, and document impacts.
5. Route for internal/client approval according to authority.
6. Update the schedule, budget, drawings/specifications, correspondence, and change log only after authorization.
7. Communicate the approved direction and retain the audit trail.

Never implement an undocumented change or treat a verbal request as approved authorization. PMI's integrated-change-control guidance emphasizes a defined process and documented roles for reviewing change requests; see the [PMBOK reference](https://www.pmi.org/-/media/pmi/documents/public/pdf/pmbok-standards/pmbok-guide-6th-edition-5th-printing.pdf).

## Construction Document Terms

- **Drawing:** graphical design information issued with a number, discipline, revision, and status.
- **Specification:** written technical and administrative requirements for materials, systems, quality, installation, and testing.
- **RFI — Request for Information:** a formal question used to clarify unclear, incomplete, or conflicting project information. Autodesk describes an RFI as a construction document used to clarify details and address project questions; see its [RFI overview](https://construction.autodesk.com/resources/checklists/rfi-template/).
- **Submittal:** contractor/supplier information submitted for review against design requirements; it may include product data, samples, calculations, schedules, or shop drawings.
- **Shop drawing:** detailed fabrication or installation information prepared by a contractor, fabricator, manufacturer, or supplier. Review status does not transfer design/installation responsibility unless the contract says otherwise.
- **Transmittal:** a formal record of documents or items sent, including sender, recipient, date, and contents.
- **Revision:** a controlled update to a document. Never overwrite the history or distribute a superseded revision as current.

Procore's official guide explains that its Submittals tool organizes logs and can include shop drawings and product-catalogue pages, while tracking status and responsible reviewers. Review the [Submittals tool overview](https://support.procore.com/products/online/user-guide/project-level/submittals) and [submittal best practices](https://support.procore.com/products/online/user-guide/project-level/submittals/best-practices-submittals).

## Submittal and Shop-Drawing Coordination

1. Confirm the required submittal against the specification/register.
2. Log its number, discipline, specification section, revision, submitter, reviewer, received date, required return date, and status.
3. Check administrative completeness: correct files, references, revision, and required attachments.
4. Route to the correct technical reviewer; do not perform unqualified engineering approval.
5. Track turnaround and follow up before the due date.
6. Record the official disposition exactly, such as reviewed, revise/resubmit, or rejected—using INVIRO's terminology.
7. Distribute the response to authorized recipients and retain the audit trail.
8. When resubmitted, preserve revision history and confirm that open comments are addressed by the reviewer.

## Procurement and Equipment Tracking

A basic equipment log may contain:

`Equipment Tag | Description | Specification Section | Supplier | Submittal | Approval | Required-on-Site Date | Lead Time | Order Status | Delivery | Issue | Owner`

Watch for long-lead items, missing approvals, substitutions, late technical information, and delivery dates that threaten installation milestones. Do not promise acceptance or alter specifications without authorized technical and commercial review.
