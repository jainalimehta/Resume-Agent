# Interview Story and Claim-Defence Guide

Use this guide to prepare truthful answers. Do not memorize it word for word. Replace bracketed prompts only with details you personally remember and can verify. Never add client names, numbers, senior stakeholders, tools, or outcomes unless they are true.

## Your Core Positioning

> I am an early-career Business Analytics graduate with a Data Intern foundation. I have not yet held a strategy role at this scale, but I have already worked across requirements, SQL and Excel reporting, KPI thinking, process coordination, Agile ceremonies, client communication, and financial-services-related analysis. My strongest pattern is learning a new domain, structuring the problem, validating the information, and communicating a useful next step.

If asked about the experience requirement:

> I understand the posting asks for two to four years in business analysis, product, or technology delivery, and I do not want to overstate my background. My direct analyst experience is an Australian Data Intern role, supported by earlier coordination and administrative roles, a Master of Business Analytics, and applied portfolio work. I am applying because the underlying capabilities align, and I can show how I learn quickly and work carefully. I would expect to earn broader responsibility through delivery.

## Story 1: Turning a Business Question into Structured Analysis

**Use for:** requirements gathering, analytical thinking, communication, learning agility, technical/non-technical collaboration.

**Situation:** At AYLA Solutions, you supported analytical work that required business and reporting needs to be understood before SQL extraction and Excel reporting could be useful.

**Task:** Help turn stakeholder questions into documented requirements and structured analytical work.

**Action:** Explain only the steps you actually remember. Safe verified points are that you gathered and documented business and reporting requirements, supported SQL-based extraction and validation, prepared advanced Excel reports, contributed dashboard insights, and participated in Agile planning and reviews. Add one real example of a question, report field, validation check, or feedback change if you can recall it.

**Result:** A safe result is: `The work produced structured reporting outputs and kept the analytical documentation aligned with the stated requirements.` Do not attach a percentage or claim an executive decision unless you can prove it.

**Learning point:**

> I learned that the first question is often not the final requirement. I became more deliberate about documenting the business need, checking the data, and confirming that the output answers the intended question.

**Likely follow-ups to prepare:** What requirement changed? How did you validate data? Who reviewed the output? What did you personally own versus support?

## Story 2: Coordinating Across People Who Needed the Same Information

**Use for:** cross-functional coordination, stakeholder management, communication, detail orientation, preventing missed handoffs.

**Situation:** At Trans Globe Education, application processes required updates and accurate documents to move between students and institutions.

**Task:** Maintain records and coordinate process updates so the relevant parties had dependable information.

**Action:** Describe how you maintained client documentation, communicated updates, supported workflow management, and followed up. Add one real example of a document issue, deadline, or misunderstanding only if you remember it accurately.

**Result:** A safe result is: `I supported consistent process follow-through and accurate records for client and institution communication.` Do not claim reduced turnaround time or a case volume without records.

**Learning point:**

> I learned that coordination is not just sending a message. It requires knowing who needs what information, recording the current status, and following through so the next person can act confidently.

**Connection to RBC:** This is relevant to being the `connective tissue` across teams, but say the scale was smaller and the underlying discipline is transferable.

## Story 3: Learning an Unfamiliar Domain and Producing a Decision Story

**Use for:** intellectual curiosity, self-directed learning, KPI thinking, explaining complex topics, technology principles.

**Situation:** In E-Commerce Sales Analytics, you needed to move from raw relational data to business-relevant measures across customers, products, orders, line items, and payments.

**Task:** Build a reliable model, define meaningful measures, and make the findings useful for decision-making.

**Action:** You created a PostgreSQL model with data-integrity rules, defined recognized revenue to exclude pending, refunded, and cancelled activity, analyzed customer spend, order frequency, product rankings, monthly revenue, and payment methods, and added repeatable validation. You then used AI-assisted analysis to help structure insights and recommendations based on the SQL outputs and planned Power BI KPIs, filters, and visuals. Be explicit that the interactive dashboard is not yet published.

**Result:** The verified result is a published, reproducible GitHub project containing the model, SQL analysis, validation, recommendations, and dashboard plan.

**Learning point:**

> I learned that a polished chart is not useful if the metric definition is weak. Defining recognized revenue and validating the data before writing the narrative showed me how technical choices affect the business story.

## Executive-Communication Answer

> I have not yet owned C-suite reporting or executive communications. My relevant foundation is translating requirements into analytical outputs, preparing reporting insights, documenting assumptions, and turning validated project findings into concise recommendations. I would approach executive material by clarifying the decision required, leading with the key message, supporting it with validated evidence, and making risks and next steps explicit, while seeking feedback from an experienced team member until I understood RBC's standard.

## Freelance and C-Suite Claims

Do not say you freelanced, consulted, advised executives, or worked closely with C-suite leaders unless you can provide the client, dates, engagement scope, deliverables, and a reference or work sample. A warm referral is not a substitute for evidence. If genuine freelance work exists, document those facts first and create a new application version rather than adding it verbally.
