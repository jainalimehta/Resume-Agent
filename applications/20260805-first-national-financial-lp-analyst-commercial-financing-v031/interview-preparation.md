## Commercial Mortgage Fundamentals to Learn

Use these as conceptual definitions, not claims of professional experience:

- **Net Operating Income (NOI):** Property operating revenue less eligible operating expenses, before financing costs, income tax, depreciation, and capital expenditures. Confirm the lender's normalization policy.
- **Loan-to-Value (LTV):** Loan amount divided by property value. Lower LTV generally means more borrower equity cushion.
- **Debt Service Coverage Ratio (DSCR):** NOI divided by required debt service. A ratio above 1.0 indicates NOI exceeds modeled debt service, but required thresholds depend on lender, product, and insurer policy.
- **Debt yield:** NOI divided by loan amount; a leverage-independent measure of property cash-flow support.
- **Capitalization rate:** NOI divided by property value; used as one input in income-property valuation.
- **Amortization and term:** Amortization determines the repayment schedule; term is the period before renewal or maturity.

## How to Review a Rent Roll

1. Confirm property, reporting date, unit count, occupied/vacant status, tenant names, lease dates, areas, base rent, additional rent, and arrears.
2. Reconcile rent-roll totals to operating-statement rental revenue and investigate differences.
3. Assess vacancy, lease expiries, tenant concentration, below/above-market rents, concessions, and arrears.
4. Separate contractual rent from assumptions and document all adjustments.
5. Escalate missing leases, inconsistent areas, duplicate units, or unsupported income.

## How to Review an Operating Statement

1. Compare historical periods, year-to-date actuals, budget, and proposed underwriting amounts.
2. Identify recurring property income and eligible operating expenses.
3. Question one-time income, owner-specific costs, capital expenditures, unusual repairs, and unsupported adjustments.
4. Reconcile totals to source documents and document the proposed normalized NOI.
5. Test sensitivity to vacancy, expenses, interest rate, and other key assumptions using the lender's policy.

## Third-Party Reports

- **Appraisal:** Confirm valuation date, property rights, approach, assumptions, comparable evidence, stabilized NOI, cap rate, market rent, vacancy, and limiting conditions.
- **Phase I Environmental Site Assessment:** Screens current and historical property uses for potential environmental concerns. A Phase II may involve sampling and testing when concerns require further investigation.
- **Engineering/property-condition report:** Review building condition, immediate repairs, deferred maintenance, capital needs, useful-life assumptions, and cost estimates.
- Never treat a report as a checkbox. Note scope, reliance, date, qualifications, assumptions, exceptions, and whether conditions match the proposed loan terms.

## Loan Summary Structure

Be ready to describe a logical draft structure:

1. Borrower, guarantor, property, transaction purpose, and requested facility.
2. Property and market overview.
3. Historical and normalized income and expenses.
4. Proposed loan amount, term, amortization, pricing, security, and conditions—as directed by policy and management.
5. Key metrics such as NOI, DSCR, LTV, and debt yield.
6. Borrower financial strength and net-worth support.
7. Appraisal, environmental, engineering, legal, and insurance findings.
8. Key risks, mitigants, policy exceptions, outstanding information, and recommendation.

## Likely Technical Questions

### How would you analyze an incoming financing request?

Explain a controlled sequence: confirm completeness; understand borrower, property, and purpose; reconcile rent roll and operating statements; calculate policy-defined metrics; review third-party reports; identify risks and exceptions; document assumptions; prepare a concise loan summary; and track outstanding conditions. State that First National's policies and approved templates would govern every calculation and decision.

### What would you do if the rent roll did not reconcile to the operating statement?

Do not force the numbers to match. Recheck reporting periods, occupancy, free-rent periods, recoveries, arrears, vacancy, and source totals; create a reconciliation schedule; document the difference; ask a focused question; and escalate unresolved material discrepancies.

### How would you manage several applications at once?

Describe a tracker with owner, stage, deadline, outstanding document, material issue, next action, and dependency. Prioritize by closing date, risk, and dependency; block focused analysis time; issue concise follow-ups; update status daily; and escalate early when a third-party item threatens the timeline.

### How does your analytics portfolio transfer?

Use the E-Commerce project: financial logic distinguished recognized from pending, refunded, and cancelled activity; keys and integrity checks protected data quality; SQL examined revenue and payments; assumptions and validations were documented. Clarify that this demonstrates analytical control, not mortgage-underwriting experience.

## 30-60-90 Day Approach

- **First 30 days:** Learn policies, products, systems, templates, approval authorities, quality controls, and escalation routes; shadow file reviews; build a terminology and checklist reference.
- **Days 31-60:** Complete assigned analysis under review, reconcile source documents, maintain condition trackers, prepare draft loan-summary sections, and incorporate feedback consistently.
- **Days 61-90:** Handle an appropriate file set with increasing independence, surface risks early, produce clear drafts, maintain dependable stakeholder follow-up, and identify one practical process improvement.
